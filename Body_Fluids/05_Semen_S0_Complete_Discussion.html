Seminal Fluid (Semen)

Learning Objectives

  • Describe the physiological contributions of the male reproductive glands (testes, seminal vesicles, prostate, and bulbourethral glands) to ejaculate composition, coagulation, and liquefaction.
  • Detail standardized pre-analytical requirements for semen collection, patient preparation, transport temperature, and specimen acceptance/rejection criteria.
  • Perform and interpret macroscopic physical parameters (appearance, volume, viscosity, liquefaction time, pH) and specialized chemical assays (fructose, zinc, acid phosphatase).
  • Calculate sperm concentration using manual hemocytometer chambers and evaluate microscopic characteristics including motility grades, viability staining, morphology, and round cell differentiation.
  • Correlate seminal fluid abnormalities with clinical pathology, including male factor infertility patterns, genitourinary tract obstructions, leukocytospermia, and post-vasectomy verification.

Physiology of Semen Production & Glandular Contributions

Core Concepts & Detailed Procedure / Concept Analysis

Semen is a specialized body fluid composed of spermatozoa suspended in seminal plasma. The final ejaculate is formed by the sequential contributions of four anatomical structures of the male reproductive system:

  • Testes & Epididymides (~5% of total volume):

    • Testes: Contain the seminiferous tubules where spermatogenesis takes place under the stimulation of follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and testosterone (produced by interstitial Leydig cells stimulated by luteinizing hormone [LH]). Spermatogenesis requires approximately 64 to 74 days.
    • Epididymides: Paired crescent-shaped organs resting on the posterior surface of each testis. Immature, non-motile spermatozoa enter from the rete testis and undergo an 8- to 14-day transit, during which they acquire forward progressive motility and mature their surface glycoproteins. Spermatozoa are stored primarily in the tail (cauda) of the epididymis until ejaculation.

  • Seminal Vesicles (60–70% of total volume):

    • Produce an elongated, viscous, alkaline fluid (pH ~7.6–8.0) that neutralizes the acidic vaginal vault.
    • Major constituent is fructose, an essential nutrient and primary glycolytic energy substrate that spermatozoa utilize for flagellar adenosine triphosphate (ATP) production and motility.
    • Provides flavin, a pigment responsible for the characteristic opalescent gray-white color and intense yellow autofluorescence under ultraviolet (UV) light.
    • Contains fibrinogen-like coagulating proteins (semenogelin I and II) that initiate the formation of a gelatinous coagulum immediately post-ejaculation.
    • Contains prostaglandins, which induce smooth muscle contractions in the female reproductive tract to facilitate sperm transport.

  • Prostate Gland (20–30% of total volume):

    • Secretes a thin, slightly acidic, milky fluid rich in citric acid, acid phosphatase (prostatic acid phosphatase [PAP]), zinc, and free proteolytic enzymes.
    • Proteolytic enzymes: Primarily prostate-specific antigen (PSA), a serine protease that cleaves semenogelins within 30 to 60 minutes after ejaculation, liquefying the seminal coagulum to liberate spermatozoa.
    • Acid phosphatase: Clinically utilized in forensic pathology as a definitive marker for the presence of semen in alleged sexual assault investigations.
    • Zinc: Serves as a biochemical stabilizer for nuclear chromatin within sperm heads and exerts antibacterial properties within the genitourinary tract.

  • Bulbourethral (Cowper) Glands (<5% of total volume):

    • Small, pea-sized glands located inferior to the prostate.
    • Release a thick, clear, alkaline mucus preceding the bulk of the ejaculate to neutralize acidic residual urine within the penile urethra and lubricate the urethral meatus.

Clinical Correlations, Diagnostics & QC

The order of ejaculation is physiologically fixed:

  1. Bulbourethral fluid (urethral flush and lubrication)
  2. Prostatic secretions mixed with epididymal sperm (concentrated sperm fraction)
  3. Seminal vesicle fluid (bulk volume and fructose vehicle)

Loss of the initial fraction during collection leads to falsely low sperm concentration counts. Conversely, loss of the terminal fraction falsely elevates sperm concentration while reducing total volume and impairing semen coagulation.

Key Takeaways & Diagnostic Pearls

  • Seminal vesicles supply the highest volume (60–70%) and fructose (energy for motility).
  • The prostate supplies PSA for liquefaction, acid phosphatase for forensic identification, and zinc.
  • The testes/epididymis contribute only 5% of the total fluid volume but provide all spermatozoa.
  • Coagulation is mediated by seminal vesicle proteins; liquefaction is mediated by prostatic proteases (PSA).

Specimen Collection, Handling & Pre-Analytical Quality Assurance

Core Concepts & Detailed Procedure / Concept Analysis

Because sperm concentration, motility, and viability exhibit significant biological and temporal fluctuations, standardized pre-analytical control is mandatory:

  • Sexual Abstinence Period:

    • Must be strictly maintained between 2 to 3 days (48–72 hours), but not exceeding 5 days.
    • Abstinence <2 days: Falsely low sperm concentrations and lower total fluid volumes.
    • Abstinence >5 days: Artificially elevated sperm concentrations with degraded motility, decreased viability, and increased morphologic abnormalities (senescent sperm accumulation).

  • Patient Collection Guidelines:

    • The specimen must be obtained via masturbation directly into a pre-warmed, clean, sterile, wide-mouthed plastic container made of non-toxic, non-spermicidal polypropylene.
    • Prohibited collection methods:
    • Coitus interruptus (withdrawal): Yields loss of the sperm-rich first fraction and introduces cellular/microbiological contamination from vaginal secretions.
    • Standard commercial latex/non-latex condoms: Contain spermicidal additives (such as nonoxynol-9) and toxic plasticizers that completely immobilize spermatozoa within minutes. Specialized, non-spermicidal silicone condoms are permitted only under strict physician guidance when religious or cultural beliefs prohibit masturbation.

  • Specimen Transport & Laboratory Receiving:

    • The specimen container must be labeled with the patient's full legal name, date of birth, medical record number, and the exact time and date of collection.
    • The patient questionnaire must record the duration of sexual abstinence and whether the entire ejaculate was captured.
    • The specimen must arrive at the testing laboratory within 1 hour (60 minutes) of collection.
    • Transport temperature must be preserved between 20°C and 37°C (ideally near body temperature, ~37°C; patients are instructed to carry the collection container close to their body inside a warm coat or pocket). Exposure to temperatures <20°C impairs motility; temperatures >40°C cause thermal death.
    • Upon laboratory receipt, the bench technologist logs the precise arrival time and places the specimen in a 37°C incubator or warm water bath to undergo complete liquefaction.

Clinical Correlations, Diagnostics & QC

Due to biological variability, a single abnormal semen analysis is never diagnostic. To establish an accurate fertility baseline, a minimum of two (and preferably three) consecutive semen analyses must be conducted at intervals of 2 to 3 weeks, never exceeding 3 months apart.

Key Takeaways & Diagnostic Pearls

  • Ideal abstinence interval: 2 to 3 days (never >5 days).
  • Acceptable transport window: Arrive in the laboratory within 1 hour (60 minutes).
  • Temperature regulation during transport: Protect from extremes; maintain between 20°C and 37°C.
  • Minimum diagnostic series: Two separate specimens collected 2–3 weeks apart.

Macroscopic Physical Examination & Chemical Testing

Core Concepts & Detailed Procedure / Concept Analysis

Macroscopic evaluation begins immediately following liquefaction.

1. Liquefaction

  • Freshly ejaculated semen forms a dense, gelatinous coagulum. Within 30 to 60 minutes, prostatic proteolytic enzymes (PSA) break down the semenogelins, transforming the fluid into a uniform, translucent liquid.
  • Prolonged or absent liquefaction (>60 minutes): Pathologic and indicative of prostate dysfunction (deficiency of prostatic enzymes).
  • Laboratory processing note: Microscopic analysis cannot proceed until liquefaction is complete. If delayed beyond 60 minutes, the specimen may be chemically liquefied for subsequent testing by adding an equal volume of physiological saline, proteolytic enzymes (e.g., alpha-chymotrypsin, bromelain), or by gentle mechanical homogenization via a blunt 19-gauge needle attached to a syringe. Such manipulations must be documented on the final laboratory report.

2. Appearance & Color

  • Normal: Homogeneous, translucent, gray-white to pearlescent opalescent.
  • Red, brown, or rust-colored (Hemospermia): Presence of red blood cells; associated with prostate trauma, urethral stricture, malignancy, or acute infection.
  • Turbid, white-opaque: Associated with high concentrations of white blood cells (leukocytospermia/infection).
  • Yellow: Prolonged abstinence, presence of urine (retrograde ejaculation or urethral contamination), jaundice (bilirubinemia), or intake of specific medications (e.g., multivitamins, sulfasalazine).

3. Ejaculate Volume

  • ASCP BOC Standard Reference Range: 2.0 – 5.0 mL (WHO lower limit: $\ge 1.5\text{ mL}$).
  • Measured using a calibrated wide-bore serological pipette or a graduated plastic centrifuge tube. (Do not use standard narrow-bore pipettes before checking viscosity).
  • Hypospermia (<2.0 mL): Associated with incomplete collection, retrograde ejaculation into the urinary bladder, ejaculatory duct obstruction, androgen deficiency, or congenital bilateral absence of the vas deferens (CBAVD).
  • Hyperspermia (>5.0 mL): Associated with prolonged sexual abstinence or active accessory gland inflammation.

4. Viscosity (Consistency)

  • Assessed after complete liquefaction by aspirating the sample into a transfer pipette and allowing it to dispense dropwise under the influence of gravity.
  • Normal viscosity: Flows in clean, discrete drops or draws out into a thin thread less than 2 cm in length.
  • Hyperviscosity (Increased viscosity): Forms persistent mucus strings or continuous strands greater than 2 cm long. Graded as 1+ (slight), 2+ (moderate), 3+ (severe).
  • Hyperviscosity impedes sperm motility, prevents even distribution in the hemocytometer, and causes sperm agglutination.

5. Seminal Fluid pH

  • ASCP BOC Standard Reference Range: 7.2 – 8.0 (Alkaline).
  • Measured within 1 hour of collection using specialized, narrow-range pH indicator paper (pH range 6.0–10.0). Exposure to room air leads to loss of $\text{CO}_2$, causing an artifactual pH elevation over time.
  • Acidic pH (<7.2): Indicative of seminal vesicle agenesis/obstruction or bilateral ejaculatory duct blockage (the acidic prostatic secretions dominate the fluid).
  • Alkaline pH (>8.0): Indicative of acute genitourinary tract infection or prostatitis.

Chemical Testing of Semen

Chemical tests are utilized to evaluate accessory gland integrity, primarily when investigating azoospermia (zero sperm present):

    • Reagent: Resorcinol in hydrochloric acid.
    • Reaction: Fructose + Resorcinol $\xrightarrow{\Delta}$ Orange-Red Precipitate.
    • Clinical scenario: An azoospermic specimen with low volume (<1.5 mL) and acidic pH (<7.2) that tests negative for fructose confirms an anatomical blockage or aplasia of the seminal vesicles.

Key Takeaways & Diagnostic Pearls

  • Normal liquefaction: 30–60 minutes at 37°C.
  • ASCP BOC Reference Ranges:
    • Volume: 2 – 5 mL
    • pH: 7.2 – 8.0
  • Seminal vesicles provide alkaline secretions and fructose; the prostate provides acidic fluid, PSA, zinc, and acid phosphatase.
  • An acidic, low-volume, fructose-negative, azoospermic specimen is the classic profile for ejaculatory duct obstruction or congenital absence of the vas deferens and seminal vesicles.

Microscopic Analysis: Concentration, Motility, Morphology & Viability

Core Concepts & Detailed Procedure / Concept Analysis

Microscopic analysis is performed only after the specimen has achieved complete liquefaction and has been thoroughly mixed.

1. Sperm Motility

  • Must be assessed within 1 hour of collection on a pre-warmed slide (37°C) under high-dry magnification (400x).
  • Count at least 200 spermatozoa across multiple random fields.
  • ASCP BOC Criteria: Normal motility is >50% motile forms within 1 hour.
  • World Health Organization (WHO) Grading System:
    • Progressive Motility (PR): Spermatozoa moving actively, linearly or in large circles, regardless of speed ($\ge 32\text{%}$ reference cutoff).
    • Non-Progressive Motility (NP): Flagellar movement without forward displacement (swimming in tiny circles, twitching in place).
    • Immotility (IM): Completely motionless ($0\text{%}$ displacement).
    • Asthenospermia: Substandard sperm motility (<50% total motility or <32% progressive motility).

2. Sperm Concentration and Total Sperm Count

  • Evaluated manually using a standard Improved Neubauer Hemocytometer.
  • Dilution Preparation:
    • A 1:20 dilution is made using an immobilizing and killing diluent (typically 5% sodium bicarbonate containing 1% formalin, or distilled water).
    • Dilution Factor (DF) = 20.
  • Hemocytometer Loading & Chamber Settling:
    • Both chambers are charged and placed inside a moist humidified Petri dish for 3 to 5 minutes to allow spermatozoa to settle into a single optical focal plane.
    • Count spermatozoa in the designated grid areas (typically the center large 1 mm$^2$ erythrocyte square or its four corner and central tertiary squares). Both sides must agree within 10%.
    • Only mature, flagellated cells with a head and tail are counted. Free pinheads (heads without tails) or isolated tails are excluded.
Standard Hemocytometer Calculation Formula:
Cells/µL = (Total Cells Counted x Dilution Factor) / (Number of Large Squares Counted x 0.1 mm Depth)

To convert to Sperm Concentration per mL:

$$\text{Sperm/mL} = \text{Sperm/}\mu\text{L} \times 1,000$$

$$\text{Total Sperm Count / Ejaculate} = \text{Sperm Concentration (sperm/mL)} \times \text{Total Ejaculate Volume (mL)}$$
Hemocytometer Calculation Demonstration:
  • Specimen volume = 3.0 mL.
  • Dilution = 1:20 (DF = 20).
  • Depth = 0.1 mm.

  • A total of 120 sperm are counted across 2 large squares (each 1 mm$^2$ area):

    $$\text{Sperm/}\mu\text{L} = \frac{120 \times 20}{2 \times 0.1} = \frac{2,400}{0.2} = 12,000\text{ sperm/}\mu\text{L}$$


    $$\text{Sperm Concentration} = 12,000\text{ sperm/}\mu\text{L} \times 1,000\text{ }\mu\text{L/mL} = 12,000,000\text{ sperm/mL} = 12.0 \times 10^6\text{ sperm/mL}$$


    $$\text{Total Sperm Count} = 12.0 \times 10^6\text{ sperm/mL} \times 3.0\text{ mL} = 36.0 \times 10^6\text{ sperm/ejaculate}$$

  • Reference Ranges:

    • ASCP BOC Standard: >20 $\times 10^6$/mL (Total sperm/ejaculate: $>40 \times 10^6$).
    • Oligospermia: Concentration $<20 \times 10^6$/mL.
    • Azoospermia: Complete absence of spermatozoa in the ejaculate (confirmed following high-speed centrifugation at 3000 $\times g$ for 15 minutes and examining the pellet).

3. Sperm Morphology

  • Thin smears are prepared, air-dried, and stained with Papanicolaou, Giemsa, or Wright-Giemsa stain, then examined under oil immersion (1000x).
  • Evaluate a minimum of 200 spermatozoa against strict structural parameters:

Morphological Region Normal Dimensions / Characteristics Common Pathologic Defects
Head Oval shape; smooth contour; length 4.0–5.0 µm; width 2.5–3.5 µm. Acrosomal cap covers 40–70% of the anterior nuclear head. Macrocephalic (giant), microcephalic, tapered (pencil-head), pyriform, duplicate (two heads), round (globozoospermia—lacks acrosome), severely vacuolated.
Neck & Midpiece Straight; aligned along the longitudinal axis of the head; length 6.0–7.0 µm, thickness ~1.0 µm. Contains mitochondria. Bent neck (>90° axis angle), asymmetric insertion, thickened/swollen midpiece, persistent cytoplasmic droplet (>one-third head size).
Tail (Principal & End Piece) Slender, uniform, uncoiled flagellum; approx. 45 µm in length. Coiled tail, broken tail, duplicate tails, short tail, terminal hairpin bends.
  • Reference Ranges:
    • ASCP BOC Standard Reference Range: >30% normal forms (routine criteria).
    • Kruger Strict Criteria: $\ge 4\text{%}$ normal forms (identifies in-vitro fertilization [IVF] success capacity).
    • Teratospermia: Excess percentage of abnormal morphologic forms.

4. Sperm Vitality (Viability Testing)

  • Performed when motility is markedly depressed (<50% motile or <32% progressive). Differentiates immotile living sperm from dead sperm.
  • Supravital Eosin-Nigrosin Dye Exclusion Principle:
    • Live sperm: Possess intact, selective plasma cell membranes that exclude eosin dye. They remain unstained (white) against the dark purple-black nigrosin background.
    • Dead sperm: Have damaged, permeable cell membranes that allow eosin to penetrate. They stain pink or bright red.
  • Reference Limit: $\ge 58\text{%}$ viable (unstained) forms.
  • Necrospermia: Specimen displaying entirely dead spermatozoa (100% dead, eosin-positive).
  • Primary Ciliary Dyskinesia (Kartagener Syndrome): Live but completely non-motile spermatozoa (eosin-negative, unstained, but motility = 0%).

5. Differentiation of Round Cells (Peroxidase Staining)

  • Undiluted semen wet mounts frequently contain non-sperm spherical cells known collectively as "round cells". These consist of:
    1. Immature male germ cells (spermatocytes, spermatids).
    2. White blood cells (WBCs / Leukocytes) (predominantly segmented neutrophils).
  • Because light microscopy cannot reliably distinguish small spermatids from neutrophils on an unstained chamber, a myeloperoxidase cytochemical stain is utilized:
    • Neutrophils express active myeloperoxidase: Stain Dark Brown / Yellow-Brown.
    • Immature germ cells and lymphocytes lack peroxidase: Remain Unstained.
Calculation for WBC Concentration:
WBCs/mL = (Number of Peroxidase-Positive Cells Counted / 100 Sperm) x Sperm Concentration (sperm/mL)
  • ASCP BOC Standard Reference Range: $<1.0 \times 10^6$ WBCs/mL ($<1.0 \times 10^9$/L).
  • Leukocytospermia (Pyospermia): Presence of $>1.0 \times 10^6$ WBCs/mL. Indicates active genitourinary inflammation or infection (prostatitis, epididymitis, urethritis) and generates damaging reactive oxygen species (ROS) that induce lipid peroxidation of the sperm membrane.

Key Takeaways & Diagnostic Pearls

  • ASCP BOC Microscopic Cutoffs:
    • Concentration: >20 $\times 10^6$/mL
    • Motility: >50% motile within 1 hour
    • Morphology: >30% normal forms
    • WBCs: $<1.0 \times 10^6$/mL
  • Eosin-nigrosin: Live sperm = White (exclude dye); Dead sperm = Pink/Red (take up dye).
  • Peroxidase staining distinguishes neutrophils (brown, peroxidase-positive) from immature round germ cells (unstained, peroxidase-negative).

Diagnostic Classifications

  • Normozoospermia: All physical, chemical, and microscopic parameters fall within standard reference intervals.
  • Oligospermia: Sperm concentration $<20 \times 10^6$/mL.
  • Asthenospermia: Motility $<50\text{%}$ motile forms (or $<32\text{%}$ progressive motility).
  • Teratospermia: Morphology $<30\text{%}$ normal forms (or $<4\text{%}$ Kruger strict criteria).
  • Oligoasthenoteratospermia (OAT): Combined deficiency across all three major semen parameters; represents the most common profile in severe male factor subfertility.
  • Azoospermia: Complete absence of spermatozoa in the ejaculate.

Post-Vasectomy Seminal Fluid Analysis

  • Clinical Purpose: Surgical sterilization via bilateral transection/ligation of the vas deferens. Post-vasectomy semen analysis confirms procedure success and safe cessation of alternative contraception.
  • Testing Protocol:
    • Conducted at 2 to 3 months post-surgery, and/or following a clearance threshold of at least 15 to 20 ejaculations.
    • The patient maintains standard 2- to 3-day abstinence.
    • Laboratory Examination:
      1. Initial wet mount direct examination of the liquefied specimen at 400x magnification.
      2. If no sperm are observed, the entire specimen is centrifuged at high speed ($\ge 3,000 \times g$ for 15 minutes).
      3. The supernatant is aspirated, and the entire sediment pellet is examined microscopically.
  • Confirmation of Success: Complete azoospermia (zero spermatozoa seen in the centrifuged pellet).
  • Persistent Motile Sperm: Indicates surgical failure, recanalization of the vas deferens, or missed duplicated duct anatomy.
  • Rare Non-Motile Sperm: If small numbers of non-motile sperm (<100,000/mL) persist after several months, clinical protocol dictates serial monitoring until complete clearance is documented.

  • Post-vasectomy clearance requires verification of azoospermia on a centrifuged pellet ($\ge 3,000 \times g$ for 15 minutes).

ASCP MLS Content Guideline Alignment

Urinalysis and Body Fluids

  • Body Fluids (e.g., CSF, Amniotic, Synovial, Serous, Semen, Feces)
    • Physical
      • Evaluation of seminal liquefaction time (normal 30–60 minutes at 37°C) and detection of delayed liquefaction due to prostatic protease insufficiency.
      • Macroscopic appearance, color assessment (normal gray-white opalescent vs. yellow, turbid, or hemospermic red-brown), and volume measurement (ASCP BOC reference interval: 2.0–5.0 mL).
      • Viscosity determination via gravity drip pipette thread formation (normal dropwise discharge or thread length <2 cm).
      • Potentiometric pH determination using narrow-range indicator strips (normal alkaline range 7.2–8.0).
    • Chemical
      • Resorcinol qualitative and quantitative fructose testing for seminal vesicle integrity.
      • Evaluation of prostatic acid phosphatase (PAP), citric acid, and zinc as indicators of prostatic function.
      • Epididymal patency evaluation utilizing neutral alpha-glucosidase activity.
    • Microscopic
      • Manual hemocytometer chamber sperm concentration counting using Neubauer grid chambers and 1:20 immobilizing dilutions.
      • Standard cell calculation formula application:
      • Conversion to sperm concentration (cells/mL) and total sperm count per ejaculate.
      • Motility categorization (progressive, non-progressive, immotile) within 60 minutes of collection (ASCP BOC normal threshold >50% motile).
      • Assessment of viability using supravital eosin-nigrosin dye exclusion (live cells exclude eosin, remaining white; dead cells take up eosin, appearing pink).
      • Stained smear morphological differential (ASCP BOC criterion >30% normal forms; Kruger strict criteria $\ge 4\text{%}$) classifying head, neck/midpiece, and tail defects.
      • Round cell differentiation using myeloperoxidase cytochemical staining to quantify white blood cells (normal <1.0 $\times 10^6$/mL) versus immature germ cells.
    • Physiology
      • Glandular contributions to seminal plasma: Seminal vesicles (60–70%, fructose, coagulum), prostate (20–30%, PSA, acid phosphatase, zinc), testes/epididymides (5%, mature spermatozoa), and bulbourethral glands (<5%, pre-ejaculatory alkaline mucus).
      • Coagulation cascade mediated by semenogelins and subsequent liquefaction dynamics mediated by prostate-specific antigen.
    • Disease States
      • Laboratory profiles of male factor infertility: Oligospermia, asthenospermia, teratospermia, and oligoasthenoteratospermia (OAT).
      • Post-testicular ductal obstruction and congenital bilateral absence of the vas deferens (CBAVD).
      • Leukocytospermia secondary to male genitourinary tract infection and chronic prostatitis.
      • Post-vasectomy evaluation protocol for confirmation of sterilization (centrifuged pellet examination at $\ge 3,000 \times g$ for 15 minutes to verify azoospermia).

Chemistry

  • Acid phosphatase enzyme determination for accessory gland assessment and forensic semen confirmation.

Clinical Hematology

  • Hemocytometer counting rules, square dimensions (1 mm$^2$, 0.1 mm depth), dilution factors, and chamber charging principles.
  • Myeloperoxidase cytochemical stain interpretation to distinguish peroxidase-positive myeloid elements from peroxidase-negative cellular lineages.

Immunology

  • Antisperm antibody (ASA) evaluation (immunobead binding assays and mixed antiglobulin reactions) in unexplained sperm agglutination.

Microbiology

  • Identification of infectious agents (e.g., Neisseria gonorrhoeae, Chlamydia trachomatis) associated with leukocytospermia, epididymitis, and accessory gland infection.

Laboratory Operations

  • Pre-analytical specimen collection protocol: Strict 2- to 3-day sexual abstinence (maximum 5 days), wide-mouth non-toxic collection containers, and avoidance of standard condoms or coitus interruptus.
  • Transport criteria: Delivery to testing facility within 1 hour maintained at controlled physiological temperatures (20–37°C).
  • Multi-specimen testing protocol: Requirement for 2 to 3 separate ejaculate collections over 2- to 3-week intervals to establish baseline fertility.
