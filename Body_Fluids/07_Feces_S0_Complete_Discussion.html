Feces

Learning Objectives

  • Describe the normal physiological mechanisms of fecal formation and establish pre-analytical collection, handling, and preservation criteria for qualitative, timed quantitative, and parasitological specimens.
  • Correlate macroscopic stool characteristics (color, consistency, form, and gross inclusions) with normal physiological turnover, dietary/medication interferences, and gastrointestinal pathology.
  • Differentiate the analytical principles, clinical applications, specimen interferences, and quality control metrics of fecal chemical analyses, including gFOBT versus FIT, the Apt test for fetal hemoglobin, and carbohydrate reduction screening.
  • Interpret microscopic findings—including fecal leukocytes, neutral and split fecal fats, and undigested muscle fibers—to distinguish between maldigestion, malabsorption, and invasive mucosal inflammatory processes.

Physiology and Specimen Collection Protocols

Core Concepts & Detailed Procedure / Concept Analysis

Feces represents the final metabolic waste product of the human digestive system, eliminated via defecation. Normal fecal formation is the culmination of precise enzymatic breakdown in the upper gastrointestinal (GI) tract coupled with controlled fluid and electrolyte reabsorption within the colon:

  • Small Intestinal Transit & Digestion: Ingested macronutrients are degraded by pancreatic enzymes (lipase, amylase, trypsin, chymotrypsin) and intestinal brush-border disaccharidases. Monosaccharides, free fatty acids, amino acids, and the bulk of ingested fluid are absorbed across the enterocyte microvilli.
  • Large Intestinal Function: Approximately 1,000 to 1,500 mL of fluid chyme enters the cecum across the ileocecal valve daily. The ascending and transverse colon absorb water and sodium while secreting potassium and bicarbonate. Under normal motility, water absorption reduces this volume to an average fecal mass of 100 to 200 g/day.
  • Normal Stool Composition:
    • Water: Approximately 75% total weight.
    • Solid Matter: Approximately 25% total weight, comprising unabsorbed dietary fiber (cellulose), sloughed mucosal epithelial cells, trace inorganic salts, and cellular biomass.
    • Microbiome Biomass: Microorganisms constitute up to 30% to 50% of the dry fecal weight. The gut microbiota synthesizes essential micronutrients (e.g., Vitamin K, biotin) and ferments unabsorbed carbohydrates into short-chain fatty acids (acetate, propionate, butyrate).
    • Stercobilin Formation: Heme catabolism yields unconjugated bilirubin, which is conjugated in hepatocytes to bilirubin diglucuronide and excreted in bile. Intestinal bacteria reduce bilirubin diglucuronide into colorless urobilinogens (mesobilirubinogen, stercobilinogen). Stercobilinogen undergoes spontaneous non-enzymatic oxidation to form stercobilin, the orange-brown pigment responsible for characteristic stool coloration.

Pre-Analytical Criteria & Rejection Parameters

  • Container Specifications: Wide-mouthed, leak-proof, non-sterile containers. Specimens must not be contaminated with urine, toilet water, or menstrual discharge. Toilet water contains chemical disinfectants, hypochlorite, and bowl deodorizers that degrade cellular structures and cause false-positive oxidation in guaiac tests.
  • Random Spot Specimens: Appropriate for qualitative chemical tests (fecal occult blood, qualitative fat screens, fecal elastase) and microscopic leukocyte screening.
  • Timed Quantitative Specimens (72-Hour Fecal Fat): Required to account for irregular gastrointestinal transit and intermittent bowel movements.
    • Patients are maintained on a standardized dietary lipid intake (typically 70–100 g fat/day) for 48 hours prior to and during the collection period.
    • The collection container must be large, leak-proof, free of interior adhesives, and pre-weighed by the laboratory.
    • All stool passed during the continuous 72-hour window is pooled, stored refrigerated (2–8°C) to inhibit bacterial lipolysis, and delivered intact for homogenization and gravimetric or spectrophotometric fat quantitation.
  • Ova and Parasites (O&P) Collection & Preservation:
    • Liquid/diarrheic stools harbor motile trophozoites (e.g., Entamoeba histolytica, Giardia duodenalis) that degenerate within 30 to 60 minutes of passage.
    • If processing within this window is unachievable, split the raw specimen immediately into a two-vial preservative system:
    • 10% Buffered Formalin: Preserves helminth ova, larvae, and protozoan cysts for wet mount concentration procedures (formalin-ethyl acetate concentration).
    • Polyvinyl Alcohol (PVA) or Zinc-Formalin/Modified PVA: Fixes cellular protein architecture and preserves protozoan trophozoites and cysts for permanent slides stained with Wheatley trichrome or iron hematoxylin.
    • Strict Negative Rule for O&P: Refrigeration is contraindicated for fresh, unpreserved O&P specimens because thermal shock immotilizes and kills fragile amoebic trophozoites, preventing demonstration of directional pseudopodial motility.

Key Takeaways & Diagnostic Pearls

  • Normal stool consists of 75% water and 25% solids, with stercobilin (oxidized stercobilinogen) imparting the normal brown color.
  • Urine contamination destroys trophozoites and false-positively alters chemical screening assays.
  • Quantitative fat analysis mandates a strict 72-hour timed collection in a pre-weighed container with dietary compliance (70–100 g fat/day).
  • Fresh liquid stools must be examined within 30 to 60 minutes for motile trophozoites or preserved immediately in a 10% formalin / PVA two-vial system; never refrigerate unpreserved O&P specimens.

Clinical Correlations, Diagnostics & QC

Macroscopic triage dictates downstream analytical routing:

  • Acholic Stools & Biliary Blockage: Complete absence of stercobilin produces acholic (clay-colored) feces. Conjugated bilirubin cannot cross the common bile duct into the duodenum, leading to conjugated hyperbilirubinemia, marked bilirubinuria, absence of urine urobilinogen, and marked elevation of serum Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP) and Gamma-Glutamyl Transferase (GGT).
  • True Melena versus Medication Artifact: True melena demonstrates a distinctive viscous, tarry consistency with an intense pungent odor resulting from bacterial decomposition of extravasated blood proteins. In contrast, bismuth and iron supplements yield firm, dry, dark gray-to-black stools lacking tarry stickiness, with negative occult blood testing.
  • Mucus Analysis: Visible mucus on unformed feces is never physiological. Translucent surface mucus correlates with spastic constipation, irritable bowel syndrome, or neurogenic colitis. Mucus admixed with frank red blood and pus confirms severe invasive mucosal ulceration (ulcerative colitis, dysentery).

Key Takeaways & Diagnostic Pearls

  • Upper GI bleeding produces black, tarry melena through the gastric acid/peptic conversion of hemoglobin to hematin.
  • Ingestion of bismuth, oral iron, or charcoal mimics melena visually (pseudo-melena) but lacks the tarry sheen and tests negative for occult blood.
  • Acholic (clay-colored) stools result from extrahepatic biliary duct obstruction, terminating hepatic biliary excretion and subsequent microbial production of stercobilin.
  • Ribbon-like stools suggest anatomical lumen narrowing, highly suspicious for obstructive rectosigmoid neoplasia.

1. Fecal Occult Blood Testing (FOBT)

Screening for occult (hidden) gastrointestinal bleeding is the primary chemical analysis performed on feces, critical for early-stage colorectal cancer detection.

2. Qualitative & Quantitative Fecal Fat Testing

Steatorrhea represents an absolute increase in fecal lipid elimination (> 7 g/24 hr on a 100 g/day dietary fat intake), resulting from either maldigestion (defective intraluminal fat processing) or malabsorption (defective mucosal transport of digested fat).

  • Qualitative Microscopic Staining (Sudan III, Sudan IV, Oil Red O):
    • Neutral Fats (Triglycerides): A fresh stool emulsion is mixed with 95% ethanol and Sudan III/IV stain. Neutral fats appear as distinct, bright orange-red to red refractile globules.
    • Reference threshold: Normal is $< 60$ neutral fat droplets per high-power field (HPF).
    • Split Fats (Free Fatty Acids and Fatty Acid Salts): A second aliquot of stool is mixed with Sudan III, treated with several drops of 36% glacial acetic acid, and heated to a gentle boil under a coverslip.
    • Heat and acid hydrolyze fatty acid salts (soaps) and ester bonds, converting them into free fatty acids that coalesce into visible orange-red droplets upon cooling.
    • Reference threshold: Normal demonstrates $< 100$ small droplets per HPF (droplet diameters $< 4\ \mu\text{m}$).
  • Quantitative Fecal Fat (Gold Standard): Performed on pooled 72-hour collections utilizing the Van de Kamer titration method or gravimetric quantification.
    • Formula: $\text{Fecal Fat Output (g/24 hr)} = \frac{\text{Total 72-hr Fecal Fat Mass (g)}}{3\text{ days}}$
    • ASCP BOC Standard Reference Range: Normal adult excretion is $\le 7.0\text{ g/24 hr}$ (coefficient of fat absorption $> 95\text{%}$).

3. Apt Test (Alkali Denaturation Test for Fetal Hemoglobin)

Used to determine the source of macroscopic blood in the stool or vomitus of a neonate: differentiating swallowed maternal blood (ingested during delivery or breastfeeding) from active neonatal GI hemorrhage.

  • Biochemical Principle: Adult hemoglobin (HbA) contains $\alpha_2\beta_2$ tetramers susceptible to alkaline denaturation. Fetal hemoglobin (HbF) contains $\alpha_2\gamma_2$ tetramers that are structurally resistant to alkali-induced ring opening and denaturation.
  • Procedure: Emulsify bloody stool or vomitus with water to liberate hemoglobin via red cell lysis. Centrifuge to obtain a clear, pink hemoglobin-containing supernatant. Add 1% Sodium Hydroxide ($\text{NaOH}$) to the pink supernatant:
    • Fetal Blood (HbF Present): Solution remains Pink after 2 minutes (alkali-resistant HbF).
    • Maternal Blood (HbA Present): Solution changes from pink to Yellow-Brown within 1 to 2 minutes due to the denaturation of HbA into alkaline hematin.

4. Carbohydrate Analysis & Fecal pH

  • Physiological Basis: Congenital or acquired disaccharidase deficiencies (e.g., lactase deficiency, celiac-induced mucosal damage) prevent carbohydrate cleavage. Unabsorbed osmotic disaccharides retain water in the intestinal lumen, producing watery osmotic diarrhea.
  • Colonic Bacterial Fermentation: Anaerobic bacteria ferment the excess luminal sugars into volatile short-chain fatty acids (lactic acid, butyric acid) and gas ($\text{H}_2, \text{CO}_2$).
  • Fecal pH Measurement:
    • Normal stool pH is neutral to slightly alkaline (pH 7.0–8.0).
    • Bacterial carbohydrate fermentation reduces fecal pH to $< 5.5$, establishing presumptive evidence of carbohydrate malabsorption.
  • Reducing Substances (Clinitest): Liquid stool is mixed 1:2 with water, centrifuged, and the supernatant is tested with a copper reduction tablet (Clinitest). A finding of $\ge 0.5\text{ g/dL}$ (or $> 0.25\text{%}$) reducing substances confirms carbohydrate malabsorption in pediatric populations. Note: Sucrose is a non-reducing sugar; it requires mild acid hydrolysis before testing if sucrose malabsorption is suspected.

Clinical Correlations, Diagnostics & QC

  • gFOBT QC Execution: Commercial guaiac card kits feature built-in positive and negative chemical on-slide control wells. The developer reagent ($\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$) must be applied to both the patient smear and the positive/negative control areas. The positive monitor must yield a distinct blue color within 30–60 seconds, while the negative monitor must remain colorless. If on-slide controls fail, patient results cannot be reported.
  • Ascorbic Acid Interference in gFOBT: Vitamin C is a strong antioxidant that directly reduces the $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ developer before it can oxidize the gum guaiac chromogen, generating a dangerous false-negative result in a bleeding patient. Ingestion of $> 250\text{ mg/day}$ of Vitamin C must be discontinued 72 hours before testing.

Key Takeaways & Diagnostic Pearls

  • gFOBT detects the heme pseudoperoxidase moiety (sensitive to upper and lower GI bleeding; susceptible to false positives from red meat/plant peroxidases and false negatives from Vitamin C).
  • FIT detects human globin (highly specific for lower GI bleeding; unaffected by dietary intake or upper GI degradation).
  • The Apt test differentiates neonatal hemorrhage from swallowed maternal blood: HbF remains Pink; HbA turns Yellow-Brown with 1% $\text{NaOH}$.
  • Fecal pH $< 5.5$ paired with a positive Clinitest ($\ge 0.5\text{ g/dL}$) on stool water confirms osmotic carbohydrate malabsorption.
  • Standard quantitative fecal fat cutoff is $\le 7.0\text{ g/24 hr}$ on a 100 g/day lipid diet.

1. Fecal Leukocytes (WBCs)

  • Technique: Prepare a thin wet mount by mixing one drop of liquid feces (or mucosal fleck) with one drop of Loeffler methylene blue or Wright/Gram stain. Cover with a coverslip and examine under high-power magnification ($400\times$).
  • Morphological Identification: Neutrophils display multi-lobed nuclei and granular cytoplasm.
  • Clinical Significance:
    • Positive Fecal Leukocytes ($\ge 3$ WBCs/HPF): Indicates direct microbial penetration, mucosal invasion, or diffuse ulceration of the intestinal wall. Pathogens include Shigella, Salmonella, Campylobacter jejuni, enteroinvasive E. coli (EIEC), and Clostridioides difficile. Non-infectious causes include active flare-ups of Inflammatory Bowel Disease (Ulcerative Colitis and Crohn disease).
    • Negative Fecal Leukocytes: Characteristic of toxin-mediated enterotoxin-driven diarrheas (Vibrio cholerae, enterotoxigenic E. coli [ETEC], Staphylococcus aureus food poisoning), viral gastroenteritis (Norovirus, Rotavirus), and non-invasive luminal parasites (Giardia duodenalis, Cryptosporidium).

3. Muscle Fibers (Creatorrhoea)

  • Technique: Emulsify stool in 10% eosin in alcohol. Eosin stains muscle protein bright red, enhancing transverse and longitudinal striations.
  • Morphological Criteria: Undigested muscle fibers demonstrate sharp, rectangular borders with distinct, prominent cross-striations (both horizontal and vertical striations clearly visible). Partially digested fibers exhibit rounded edges with faint striations; completely digested fibers appear amorphous and featureless.
  • Clinical Utility: The presence of $> 10$ undigested, striated muscle fibers indicates creatorrhoea. This points to a severe deficiency in pancreatic proteolytic enzyme production (trypsinogen, chymotrypsin) seen in advanced chronic pancreatitis and cystic fibrosis.

Clinical Correlations, Diagnostics & QC

  • Cellular Integrity and Delay Warnings: Fecal leukocytes rapidly lyse in hypotonic or warm stool specimens. Delays $> 2$ hours produce false-negative leukocyte readings due to nuclear degeneration. If processing is delayed, fix smears in methanol for subsequent Wright-Giemsa evaluation, or measure Fecal Calprotectin or Fecal Lactoferrin via quantitative ELISA. Calprotectin and lactoferrin are stable neutrophil granule proteins unaffected by cellular lysis.
  • Ova & Parasite Examination Pearls:
    • Formed stools typically contain protozoan cysts; diarrheic stools typically harbor motile trophozoites.
    • Direct saline/iodine wet mounts enable visualization of helminth eggs (e.g., Ascaris lumbricoides, Enterobius vermicularis, hookworm) and protozoan cysts (e.g., Entamoeba histolytica [1–4 nuclei with central karyosome], Giardia [oval, 4 nuclei, central axostyle]).
    • Permanent trichrome stains are required to definitively differentiate pathogenic Entamoeba histolytica from morphologically identical non-pathogenic Entamoeba dispar or commensal Entamoeba coli (up to 8 nuclei with eccentric karyosomes).

Key Takeaways & Diagnostic Pearls

  • Fecal leukocytes (neutrophils) differentiate invasive bacterial diarrhea (Shigella, Salmonella, C. jejuni) from toxigenic diarrhea (V. cholerae, ETEC, viral pathogens).
  • The Sudan III direct mount detects neutral fats (elevated in maldigestion). The acidified, heated mount hydrolyzes soaps into free fatty acids (elevated in malabsorption).
  • Undigested muscle fibers showing visible cross-striations confirm absent or severely depressed pancreatic protease output.
  • Fecal Calprotectin/Lactoferrin immunoassay acts as a stable, non-degrading molecular surrogate marker for neutrophil-mediated inflammatory bowel disease.

ASCP MLS Content Guideline Alignment

Urinalysis and Body Fluids

  • Body Fluids (e.g., CSF, Amniotic, Synovial, Serous, Semen, Feces)
    • Physical
      • Evaluation of macroscopic stool color (normal stercobilin brown, upper GI melena, lower GI hematochezia, post-hepatic acholic/clay-colored obstruction, rapid-transit green).
      • Analysis of consistency and form (pellet-like constipation, liquid diarrhea, bulky/greasy steatorrhea, mechanical obstruction ribbon-like/pencil stools).
      • Recognition and clinical correlation of blood-streaked mucus with inflammatory bowel diseases and invasive dysentery.
    • Chemical
      • Methodological principles of the Guaiac Fecal Occult Blood Test (gFOBT; heme pseudoperoxidase oxidation of guaiac) and Fecal Immunochemical Test (FIT; human globin-specific immunoassay).
      • Pre-analytical interferences in occult blood screening (dietary animal hemoglobin, plant peroxidases, ascorbic acid reducing agent inhibition).
      • Qualitative microscopic evaluation of neutral lipids versus split fatty acids using Sudan III/IV staining with acetic acid and thermal hydrolysis.
      • Standard 72-hour quantitative fecal fat reference values ($\le 7.0\text{ g/24 hr}$ on standardized $100\text{ g/day}$ dietary lipid intake).
      • Differential identification of neonatal versus swallowed maternal blood via the Apt alkali denaturation test ($1\text{%}\ \text{NaOH}$; HbF alkali-resistant pink vs. HbA denatured yellow-brown).
      • Fecal carbohydrate screening via copper reduction (Clinitest $\ge 0.5\text{ g/dL}$) and potentiometric pH determination ($\text{pH} < 5.5$) for disaccharidase deficiency.
    • Microscopic
      • Staining and enumeration of fecal polymorphonuclear neutrophils (Loeffler methylene blue wet mounts; $\ge 3\ \text{WBC/HPF}$ indicative of invasive inflammation).
      • Identification of undigested, cross-striated skeletal muscle fibers using $10\text{%}$ alcoholic eosin ($> 10\ \text{fibers}$ indicating creatorrhoea and pancreatic exocrine insufficiency).
      • Microscopic screening for neutral fats and split fatty acid globules under high-power fields.
      • Morphological discrimination of protozoan cysts, trophozoites, and helminth ova in direct saline/iodine mounts and permanent trichrome preparations.
    • Physiology
      • Intestinal enzymatic digestion, carbohydrate fermentation, mucosal transport, and colonic water/electrolyte reabsorption dynamics ($1,500\text{ mL}$ chyme reduced to $100\text{--}200\text{ g}$ stool).
      • Endogenous production of stercobilin through bacterial enzymatic reduction of biliary bilirubin diglucuronide to stercobilinogen followed by non-enzymatic spontaneous oxidation.
    • Disease States
      • Differentiation of maldigestion (pancreatic exocrine deficiency, cystic fibrosis) from mucosal malabsorption syndromes (celiac disease, tropical sprue).
      • Etiological distinction between invasive bacterial enteritis/IBD (fecal leukocyte positive) and toxin-mediated/viral enteropathy (fecal leukocyte negative).
      • Localization of gastrointestinal hemorrhage (acid hematin conversion in melena vs. unaltered lower intestinal hematochezia).
      • Diagnostic correlation of biliary atresia and post-hepatic choledocholithiasis with acholic stools.

Chemistry

  • Enzymes and Organ Function Panels
    • Correlation of acholic stools with elevated serum direct (conjugated) bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase (ALP), and gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT).
    • Assessment of pancreatic insufficiency via fecal elastase-1 quantification and 72-hour quantitative fecal lipid output.

Clinical Hematology

  • Erythrocytes and Hemoglobin Variants
    • Alkali denaturation resistance characteristics of fetal hemoglobin ($\alpha_2\gamma_2$ resistance to $1\text{%}\ \text{NaOH}$) versus adult hemoglobin ($\alpha_2\beta_2$ degradation).
    • Peripheral blood indices and iron studies (microcytic hypochromic anemia, decreased serum ferritin, elevated TIBC) secondary to chronic GI occult blood loss.

Immunology

  • Immunoassays
    • Qualitative and semi-quantitative monoclonal antibody capture assays for human fecal globin (FIT).
    • Immunoenzymatic quantification of fecal neutrophil degranulation biomarkers (fecal calprotectin and lactoferrin) for monitoring inflammatory bowel disease.

Microbiology

  • Parasitology & Enteric Pathogens
    • Collection, preservation, and staining protocols for enteric protozoa and helminths (formalin-ethyl acetate concentration, PVA-Wheatley trichrome stain).
    • Differentiation of invasive bacterial pathogens (Shigella, Salmonella, Campylobacter) from toxigenic etiologies (Vibrio cholerae, ETEC, Staphylococcus aureus enterotoxin).

Laboratory Operations

  • Pre-Analytical Specimen Triage & Quality Control
    • Criteria for specimen rejection: urine contamination, toilet water chemical interference, prolonged ambient delay ($> 60\ \text{minutes}$) for unpreserved diarrheic specimens.
    • Timed 72-hour specimen integrity, container pre-weighing, and cold-chain maintenance ($2\text{--}8^\circ\text{C}$).
    • Internal and external quality control execution for chemical occult blood test cards and colorimetric monitors.
