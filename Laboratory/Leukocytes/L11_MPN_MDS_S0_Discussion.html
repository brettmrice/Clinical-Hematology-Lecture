Routine Testing: Automated Indices & Smear Morphology

Learning Objectives

  • Differentiate the underlying pathophysiology and hematologic manifestations of Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (MPNs; effective proliferation) versus Myelodysplastic Syndromes (MDSs; ineffective hematopoiesis).
  • Evaluate automated complete blood count with differential (CBCD) parameters, flags, and red blood cell (RBC) indices to recognize suspected chronic myeloid disorders.
  • Distinguish the peripheral blood smear (PBS) morphology of the classic MPN subtypes: Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML), Polycythemia Vera (PV), Essential Thrombocythemia (ET), and Primary Myelofibrosis (PMF).
  • Identify and classify morphological hallmarks of trilineage dysplasia (dyserythropoiesis, dysgranulopoiesis, and dysmegakaryopoiesis) on Wright-Giemsa-stained peripheral blood films.
  • Apply mandatory laboratory calculations and bench troubleshooting protocols for automated count interferences, including nucleated red blood cells (nRBCs) and pseudothrombocytopenia.

Pathophysiologic Mechanisms: MPN vs MDS

Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (MPNs) and Myelodysplastic Syndromes (MDSs) are clonal hematopoietic stem cell (HSC) disorders arising from acquired somatic mutations in multipotent myeloid progenitors. Despite a shared origin within the marrow compartment, their underlying cellular kinetics diverge fundamentally.

  • Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (Effective Proliferation):
    • Autonomous, growth factor-independent proliferation across one or more committed myeloid lineages (granulocytic, erythroid, or megakaryocytic).
    • Intramedullary apoptosis is suppressed; cell maturation proceeds effectively to terminal stages.
    • Overproduction within the marrow space spills into systemic circulation, manifesting as peripheral cytoses (marked leukocytosis, absolute erythrocytosis, or severe thrombocytosis).
  • Myelodysplastic Syndromes (Ineffective Hematopoiesis):
    • Disordered lineage differentiation coupled with accelerated intramedullary apoptosis.
    • Progenitor cells proliferate within the marrow cavity, but structural and metabolic defects cause these abnormal precursors to undergo programmed cell death prior to peripheral release.
    • The marrow is characteristically hypercellular or normocellular, while peripheral blood reveals progressive, refractory cytopenias accompanied by prominent dysplastic morphology.

Complete Blood Count & Morphology of Classic MPNs

The four classic MPNs display distinctive automated analyzer profiles and blood film morphologies that reflect the primary lineage involved.

1. Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML)

  • Automated CBC Profile: Marked leukocytosis, frequently exceeding $50.0\text{ to }100.0 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$ (reference interval: $3.6\text{--}10.6 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$). Absolute basophilia ($> 0.2 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$) is an essential diagnostic feature, accompanied by absolute eosinophilia ($> 0.3 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$). Platelet counts are elevated ($> 450 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$) in approximately $50\text{%}$ of cases. Mild-to-moderate normocytic, normochromic anemia is typical.
  • Peripheral Smear Morphology:
    • Complete Granulocytic Maturation Pyramid: Smears display every stage of granulocytic differentiation from myeloblasts through mature segmented neutrophils. Segmented neutrophils and bands comprise the largest fractions.
    • "Myelocyte Bulge": A diagnostic shift where the percentage of myelocytes and metamyelocytes exceeds that of promyelocytes and occasionally approaches segmented neutrophils, disrupting standard physiological kinetics.
    • Blast Phase Stratification: In the chronic phase, peripheral myeloblasts constitute $< 10\text{%}$ of circulating leukocytes. Accelerated phase is defined by $10\text{--}19\text{%}$ blasts, while $\ge 20\text{%}$ marks transformation to acute blast phase (acute leukemia).

2. Polycythemia Vera (PV)

  • Automated CBC Profile: Absolute, autonomous erythrocytosis. Hemoglobin levels exceed $16.5\text{ g/dL}$ in men or $16.0\text{ g/dL}$ in women; hematocrit values exceed $49\text{%}$ in men or $48\text{%}$ in women (reference intervals: HGB $12.0\text{--}18.0\text{ g/dL}$, HCT $35\text{--}50\text{%}$). Panmyelosis is typical, with moderate neutrophilia ($10.0\text{--}20.0 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$) and thrombocytosis ($> 450 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$).
  • Peripheral Smear Morphology: Severe erythrocyte crowding across all microscopic fields. Red cells are predominantly normocytic and normochromic. If chronic therapeutic phlebotomy or occult bleeding depletes iron reserves, cells transition to microcytic and hypochromic ($\text{MCV} < 76\text{ fL}$, $\text{MCHC} < 32\text{ g/dL}$).

3. Essential Thrombocythemia (ET)

  • Automated CBC Profile: Sustained, unexplained thrombocytosis ($> 450 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$, frequently surpassing $1000 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$; reference interval: $150\text{--}450 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$). Hemoglobin and hematocrit remain within standard reference limits, which is the primary parameter differentiating ET from PV.
  • Peripheral Smear Morphology: Dense sheets, clumps, and clusters of platelets. Marked anisocytosis is present, including giant platelets (exceeding the diameter of normal red cells), agranular forms, and irregular pseudopodia. Circulating megakaryocytic fragments or bare dwarf nuclei may appear along slide edges.

4. Primary Myelofibrosis (PMF)

  • Automated CBC Profile: Variable based on disease progression. Early pre-fibrotic stages mimic ET with thrombocytosis and leukocytosis. Advanced fibrotic stages produce progressive cytopenias (normocytic anemia, leukopenia, and thrombocytopenia). Automated flags for immature granulocytes (IG) and nRBCs are consistently triggered.
  • Peripheral Smear Morphology (Leukoerythroblastic Triad):
    1. Dacrocytes (Teardrop Cells): Prominent poikilocytes formed as erythrocytes squeeze through rigid marrow reticulin meshworks or undergo abnormal splenic remodeling.
    2. Circulating Nucleated Red Blood Cells (nRBCs): Erythroblasts released prematurely into circulation due to disrupted bone marrow architecture.
    3. Immature Granulocytic Precursors: Left shift including myelocytes, metamyelocytes, and promyelocytes out of proportion to the degree of anemia.

Morphological Identification of Trilineage Dysplasia in MDS

The diagnosis of MDS requires high-magnification ($100\times$ oil immersion) identification of morphological atypia. Standard diagnostic criteria stipulate that $\ge 10\text{%}$ of cells within a specific lineage (erythroid, granulocytic, or megakaryocytic) must display dysplastic features.

1. Dyserythropoiesis (Erythroid Lineage)

  • Oval Macrocytes: Large, oval erythrocytes with an elevated $\text{MCV} > 100\text{ fL}$. Unlike the uniform, round macrocytes associated with liver disease or reticulocytosis, dysplastic macrocytes are distinctly oval and asymmetrical.
  • Dimorphic RBC Population: Coexistence of two distinct erythrocyte populations (e.g., macrocytic normochromic mixed with microcytic hypochromic cells) in a non-transfused patient, resulting in an elevated $\text{RDW} > 14.5\text{%}$ and a bimodal RBC volume histogram.
  • Coarse Basophilic Stippling: Dense, blue-to-purple ribosomal RNA precipitates distributed throughout the cytoplasm.
  • Pappenheimer Bodies: Small, irregular, iron-containing granules situated near the cell periphery on Wright-Giemsa stains.
  • Dysplastic Circulating nRBCs: Erythroblasts exhibiting megaloblastoid chromatin (nuclear-cytoplasmic asynchrony), multinucleation, nuclear budding, internuclear bridging, and karyorrhexis.

2. Dysgranulopoiesis (Granulocytic Lineage)

  • Pseudo-Pelger-Huët Anomaly: Acquired hyposegmentation where mature neutrophils possess bilobed nuclei connected by a thin thread ("pince-nez" spectacles) or unsegmented round/peanut-shaped nuclei. Crucially, the chromatin is condensed, clumping normally for a mature cell, distinguishing them from immature bands or metamyelocytes.
  • Neutrophil Hypogranulation / Agranulation: Pale, washed-out, gray-blue cytoplasm resulting from the partial or total absence of secondary (specific) granules.
  • Bizarre Nuclear Architecture: Dysplastic hypersegmentation ($\ge 6$ lobes), ring-shaped ("doughnut") nuclei containing a central cytoplasmic hole, and detached chromatin fragments.

3. Dysmegakaryopoiesis (Megakaryocytic Lineage)

  • Giant Platelets: Circulating platelets exceeding $7\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ in diameter, often matching or exceeding the size of erythrocytes.
  • Agranular / "Ghost" Platelets: Large platelets devoid of normal purple azurophilic $\alpha$-granules, appearing as faint, hazy blue structures.
  • Circulating Micromegakaryocytes: Naked, hypercondensed megakaryocytic nuclei or small, dwarf megakaryocytes sloughed into peripheral circulation.

1. Corrected WBC for Nucleated Red Blood Cells

Automated hematology analyzers using electronic impedance cannot distinguish nucleated red blood cells from intact leukocytes because the lytic reagents dissolve the RBC membrane but leave the nRBC nucleus intact. These unlysed nuclei pass through the aperture and are counted as leukocytes, falsely elevating the total WBC count.

  • Mandatory Action: When manual differential counts reveal $> 10\text{ nRBCs / 100 WBCs}$, the reported WBC count must be corrected:

    $$\text{Corrected WBC} = \text{Uncorrected WBC} \times \left(\frac{100}{100 + \text{nRBC}}\right)$$

  • Calculation Example: An automated analyzer reports a $\text{WBC} = 45.0 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$. The manual smear differential reveals $25\text{ nRBCs / 100 WBCs}$:

    $$\text{Corrected WBC} = 45.0 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L} \times \left(\frac{100}{100 + 25}\right) = 45.0 \times \left(\frac{100}{125}\right) = 45.0 \times 0.80 = 36.0 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$$

2. Citrate Tube Adjustment for Pseudothrombocytopenia

In patients with EDTA-dependent platelet agglutinins (cold-reacting autoantibodies that recognize neoepitopes exposed by EDTA chelation), in vitro aggregation occurs, spuriously lowering automated platelet counts and causing "platelet clumps" flags.

  • Mandatory Action:
    1. Inspect the feathered edge of the peripheral blood smear to confirm platelet clumping or platelet satellitism around neutrophils.
    2. Request a redraw collected in a $3.2\text{%}$ buffered sodium citrate tube (light blue top).
    3. Analyze the citrate sample and multiply the measured count by $1.1$ to correct for the $9:1$ blood-to-liquid anticoagulant dilution:

      $$\text{Corrected Citrate Platelet Count} = \text{Measured Citrate Platelet Count} \times 1.1$$

3. Resolving Interferences from Extreme Leukocytosis

In CML cases presenting with extreme leukocytosis ($> 50.0\text{ to }100.0 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$), two major analytical interferences occur:

  • Coincidence Loss: High cellular concentrations result in multiple cells passing through the impedance aperture simultaneously, falsely elevating RBC counts and cell sizing metrics. A manual dilution with isotonic diluent must be analyzed to obtain accurate cell counts.
  • Optical Turbidity in Hemoglobin Measurement: High leukocyte concentrations cause optical turbidity in the spectrophotometric cyanmethemoglobin/modified hemoglobin chamber, falsely elevating absorbance readings and generating an unphysiologic Mean Corpuscular Hemoglobin Concentration ($\text{MCHC} > 36\text{ g/dL}$).
  • Remediation Protocol: Centrifuge an aliquot of the whole blood sample, measure the absorbance of the clear plasma supernatant (plasma blank), and subtract that reading from the initial whole-blood hemoglobin measurement prior to recalculating indices.

4. Absolute Leukocyte Count Calculations

Clinical assessments of neutropenia or basophilia must rely on absolute concentrations rather than relative percentages:

$$\text{Absolute Cell Count} = \text{Total WBC Count} \times \left(\frac{\text{Relative Differential %}}{100}\right)$$
  • An absolute neutrophil count ($\text{ANC}$) $< 1.7 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$ establishes true neutropenia in MDS.
  • An absolute basophil count $> 0.2 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$ establishes absolute basophilia, supporting a diagnosis of CML or other MPNs over a reactive leukocytosis.

5. Rule of Three Quality Control Verification

For specimens displaying normocytic, normochromic erythrocytes, automated parameters must adhere to the mathematical relationship:

$$\text{HCT (%)} = \text{HGB (g/dL)} \times 3 \pm 3\text{%}$$
  • In classic MPNs, this relationship is maintained unless iron deficiency secondary to therapeutic phlebotomy induces microcytosis ($\text{MCV} < 76\text{ fL}$). In MDS, oval macrocytosis ($\text{MCV} > 100\text{ fL}$) and dimorphic populations disrupt this ratio, requiring verification using manual spun microhematocrit determinations.

Reflex & Confirmatory Testing Pathways

Learning Objectives

  • Differentiate the diagnostic utility, specimen handling, and microscopic morphology of bone marrow aspirates versus core biopsies.
  • Calculate, interpret, and troubleshoot the Leukocyte Alkaline Phosphatase (LAP) cytochemical score to distinguish Chronic Myeloid Leukemia from a reactive leukemoid reaction.
  • Explain the biochemical principle, microscopic evaluation, and diagnostic thresholds of Perls' Prussian blue staining for ring sideroblasts.
  • Evaluate the histochemical silver impregnation grading of bone marrow reticulin fibrosis in Primary Myelofibrosis.
  • Compare the clinical indications, tube types, and analytical targets of cytogenetic karyotyping, quantitative RT-PCR for $BCR\text{-}ABL1$, targeted MPN mutation panels ($JAK2$, $CALR$, $MPL$), and multi-gene MDS Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS).
  • Construct a pre-analytical specimen triaging workflow that directs whole blood, bone marrow aspirates, and core biopsies to their respective testing destinations while avoiding anticoagulant interferences.

Diagnostic Evaluation Parameters

  1. Cellularity Quantification: Evaluated on the core biopsy by calculating the area occupied by hematopoietic precursors versus clear adipocytes. Physiological expected cellularity is age-dependent:

    $$\text{Expected Normal Cellularity (%)} \approx 100 - \text{Patient's Age}$$
    • MPNs: Strikingly hypercellular ($80\text{--}>90\text{%}$), reflecting panmyelosis.
    • MDS: Paradoxically hypercellular or normocellular in $80\text{--}90\text{%}$ of cases, contrasting sharply with the peripheral cytopenias. Hypocellular MDS occurs in $< 10\text{%}$ of presentations.
      2. Megakaryocyte Morphology & Distribution:
    • MPNs: Marked hyperplasia. In ET and pre-fibrotic PMF, megakaryocytes form dense clusters and display giant, deeply folded, "staghorn-like" hyperlobated nuclei. In CML, megakaryocytes are typically smaller than normal ("dwarf" forms) with hypolobated nuclei, but lack dysplastic atypia.
    • MDS: Dysmegakaryopoiesis is a diagnostic hallmark. Megakaryocytes are dispersed, presenting as micromegakaryocytes (dwarf forms matching promyelocytes in diameter) or displaying widely separated, unlobulated multiple nuclei ("paw-print" or "pigs-in-a-pen" appearance).
      3. Blast Percentage Enumeration: Determined by performing a manual 500-cell nucleated differential count on particle-rich aspirate smears:
    • MPNs: Blasts remain $< 10\text{%}$ in chronic phases. Progressive increases between $10\text{--}19\text{%}$ denote the accelerated phase, while $\ge 20\text{%}$ marks acute transformation.
    • MDS: The blast count is the primary parameter for risk stratification and classification. Marrow blasts remain $< 20\text{%}$ by definition. Proportions between $5\text{--}19\text{%}$ define MDS with Excess Blasts (MDS-EB), signaling an elevated risk of progression to Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML).
      4. "Dry Tap" Troubleshooting: When severe reticulin fibrosis (e.g., overt PMF) or dense cellular proliferation prevents liquid marrow aspiration (a "dry tap"), touch imprints must be prepared immediately. Unfixed core biopsy fragments are rolled along clean glass slides, air-dried, and stained with Wright-Giemsa to preserve single-cell morphology for blast differential counts.

Cytochemical Stains: LAP & Perls' Prussian Blue

1. Leukocyte Alkaline Phosphatase (LAP) Scoring

Leukocyte Alkaline Phosphatase is an intracellular hydrolytic enzyme located in the secondary (specific) granules of maturing neutrophils (bands and segmented neutrophils).

(pH 9.5 - 9.7)"] -->|"Alkaline Phosphatase

Scoring Procedure & Calculation

  • Smears prepared from fresh capillary fingersticks or heparinized whole blood are fixed in cold ($0\text{--}4^\circ\text{C}$) formalin-methanol, incubated in substrate-diazonium solution at $\text{pH } 9.5\text{--}9.7$, and counterstained with hematoxylin.
  • Under $1000\times$ oil-immersion microscopy, 100 consecutive bands and segmented neutrophils are scored from $0$ to $4+$ based on precipitate density:
    • $0$: No granular precipitate; clear cytoplasm.
    • $1+$: Faint, rare, scattered granules.
    • $2+$: Moderate, discrete granulation.
    • $3+$: Dense granulation; nucleus remains distinct.
    • $4+$: Deep, confluent precipitate obscuring the nucleus.
  • Calculation: The score is the sum of the products of each cell count and its grade:

    $$\text{LAP Score} = \sum (\text{Grading Score } [0\text{ to }4] \times \text{Cell Count})$$


    $$\text{Theoretical Score Range} = 0 \text{ to } 400$$

LAP Result Range Clinical Conditions
Markedly Decreased $< 20$ Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML), Paroxysmal Nocturnal Hemoglobinuria (PNH)
Normal $20\text{--}130$ (up to $160$) Healthy baseline state
Markedly Elevated $> 160$ Leukemoid Reaction (severe sepsis), Polycythemia Vera (PV), 3rd trimester pregnancy
  • Bench Considerations: Neutrophilic alkaline phosphatase is labile. Smears must be fixed promptly. EDTA anticoagulation inhibits LAP activity over time via heavy metal chelation; fresh capillary smears or heparinized blood are required. While molecular testing for $BCR\text{-}ABL1$ provides definitive objective identification of CML, the LAP score remains an exam classic for resolving marked leukocytosis.

2. Perls' Prussian Blue Cytochemical Iron Reaction

Perls' Prussian blue stain visualizes non-heme ferric iron ($\text{Fe}^{3+}$) reserves in marrow aspirate spicules.

  • Analytical Specificity: Functional iron coordinated within the protoporphyrin ring of hemoglobin and ferrous ($\text{Fe}^{2+}$) iron do not react. Smears are counterstained with Nuclear Fast Red.
  • Microscopic Evaluation:
    • Macrophage Storage Iron: Graded $0\text{ to }4+$ to assess systemic reserves. Absent ($0$) indicates iron deficiency anemia; elevated ($3+\text{ to }4+$) indicates anemia of chronic inflammation or secondary iron overload.
    • Normal Sideroblasts: Developing intermediate and late normoblasts containing $1\text{ to }4$ fine Prussian-blue granules scattered randomly in the cytoplasm ($20\text{--}50\text{%}$ of normoblasts in healthy marrow).
    • Pathological Ring Sideroblasts: Erythroblasts with $\ge 5$ coarse Prussian blue granules encircling at least one-third of the nucleus circumference. These granules represent non-heme iron accumulated within the cristae of swollen perinuclear mitochondria due to defective heme biosynthesis.
  • Diagnostic Thresholds: Under standard classification, a diagnosis of MDS with Ring Sideroblasts (MDS-RS) requires:
    • $\ge 15\text{%}$ ring sideroblasts among marrow erythroid precursors.
    • OR $\ge 5\text{%}$ ring sideroblasts if accompanied by a documented mutation in the spliceosome gene $SF3B1$.

Histochemical Silver Staining & Flow Cytometry Indications

1. Reticulin Silver Impregnation for Marrow Fibrosis

Reticulin is an extracellular matrix protein composed predominantly of Type III collagen. Because delicate reticulin fibers are invisible on regular aspirate smears or standard H&E sections, silver impregnation (e.g., Gomori's) is performed on core biopsy sections. Silver ions bind to reticulin and are reduced to metallic silver by formalin, yielding a black outline of the stromal network.

2. Indications for Flow Cytometry Referral

When CBCD flags and peripheral smears show unexplained cytopenias, dysplastic granulocytes, or circulating blasts, an unfixed bone marrow aspirate aliquot collected in sodium heparin or EDTA must be referred for multiparameter flow cytometry.

  • CD34 and CD117 Progenitor Gating: Precise quantification of blast fractions relative to total CD45+ events.
  • Immunophenotypic Dysplasia: Detection of lineage infidelity, asynchronous antigen co-expression (e.g., aberrant CD7 or CD56 on myeloid blasts), or aberrant antigen loss (e.g., loss of CD13, CD33, or CD45) to establish clonality.

1. Conventional Cytogenetics (Karyotyping)

  • Specimen Requirements: Viable, living cells collected in Sodium Heparin (green top). EDTA is unacceptable because it chelates divalent cations essential for tissue culture and kills dividing cells.
  • Analytical Workflow: Mitogenic stimulation $\rightarrow$ colcemid mitotic spindle arrest $\rightarrow$ hypotonic KCl nuclear swelling $\rightarrow$ methanol-acetic acid fixation $\rightarrow$ slide preparation $\rightarrow$ trypsin-Giemsa G-banding. Analysis evaluates $\ge 20$ metaphase spreads.
  • Key Interpretations:
    • CML: Reciprocal balanced translocation $\text{t}(9;22)(\text{q}34.1;\text{q}11.2)$, generating the Philadelphia ($\text{Ph}$) chromosome.
    • MDS: Clonal structural/numerical abnormalities occur in $\sim 50\text{%}$ of primary cases, guiding Revised International Prognostic Scoring System (IPSS-R) stratification:
    • Deletion 5q $[\text{del}(5\text{q})]$: Favorable prognosis, responsive to lenalidomide.
    • Monosomy 7 $[-7]$ or $\text{del}(7\text{q})$: High-risk transformation to AML; adverse prognosis.
    • Trisomy 8 $[+8]$: Intermediate risk category.

2. Quantitative RT-PCR for $BCR\text{-}ABL1$

  • Specimen Requirements: Whole blood or marrow collected in EDTA (purple top). RNA is labile; samples must be stabilized or refrigerated at $2\text{--}8^\circ\text{C}$ and processed within 24 to 48 hours.
  • Analytical Utility: Converts RNA to cDNA via reverse transcriptase, followed by real-time qPCR amplification.
    • Diagnosis: Detects cryptic translocations that escape detection on routine G-banded karyograms.
    • Monitoring: Measures minimal residual disease (MRD) in CML patients on tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) therapy. Achieving a Major Molecular Response (MMR) is defined as a $\ge 3\text{-log}$ reduction ($\le 0.1\text{%}$ on the International Scale) in $BCR\text{-}ABL1$ transcript levels.

3. Targeted Driver Mutation Assays: $JAK2$, $CALR$, $MPL$

Conducted via allele-specific real-time PCR or Sanger sequencing on EDTA whole blood/aspirate:

  • $JAK2$ V617F: A single-nucleotide point mutation ($\text{c}.1849\text{G}>\text{T}$) in exon 14 causing a substitution of phenylalanine for valine at codon 617, driving constitutive kinase activity. Negative testing in PV triggers reflex analysis for $JAK2$ exon 12 mutations.
  • $CALR$: Insertions or deletions (indels) in exon 9.
  • $MPL$: Point mutations in exon 10 (e.g., W515K/L).

4. Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS) Panels

High-depth parallel sequencing of targeted myeloid gene panels using genomic DNA from EDTA specimens:

  • MDS Evaluation: Detects clonal somatic mutations in morphologically ambiguous cases with normal karyotypes. Mutations in $SF3B1$ associate with ring sideroblasts and a favorable prognosis. In contrast, mutations in $TP53$ associate with therapy-related disease, complex karyotypes, and adverse survival. Other frequent targets include $TET2$, $ASXL1$, and $RUNX1$.
  • MPN Evaluation: Identifies clonal architecture in "triple-negative" ET and PMF cases. Detection of high-risk mutations ($ASXL1$, $SRSF2$) predicts transformation into secondary acute leukemia.

Pre-Analytical Pitfalls & Laboratory Troubleshooting

  • Heparin Inhibition of Molecular Assays: Sodium heparin inhibits the Taq DNA polymerase enzyme used in PCR amplification. If an aspirate collected in heparin is submitted for BCR-ABL1 PCR or JAK2 mutation analysis, the laboratory must reject the sample and request an EDTA specimen.
  • EDTA Cytotoxicity in Cytogenetic Cultures: EDTA chelates divalent cations required for cell division and is toxic to living cells over extended periods. Karyotyping requests submitted in EDTA must be rejected due to high risk of culture failure.
  • Formalin Vapor Artifact: Aspirate smear slides stored or shipped in the same sealed container as formalin-fixed biopsy jars absorb formalin vapor. This cross-contamination alters cellular proteins, causing Romanowsky and Prussian blue stains to fail (cells appear diffuse green/gray with complete loss of nuclear detail). Smears must always be packaged separately from formalin containers.

Key Takeaways & Diagnostic Pearls

  • Effective vs Ineffective Kinetics: MPNs present as a hypercellular bone marrow accompanied by circulating cytosis ("factory in overdrive"). MDS presents as a hypercellular marrow accompanied by peripheral cytopenia ("factory discarding defective goods").
  • Classic MPN Distinctions:
    • CML: Marked leukocytosis, complete granulocytic continuum, "myelocyte bulge", absolute basophilia, and low LAP score ($< 20$); confirmed by the $t(9;22)$ translocation generating $BCR\text{-}ABL1$.
    • PV: Absolute polycythemia, panmyelosis, elevated LAP score ($> 160$); driven by $JAK2$ mutations ($> 95\text{%}$ V617F; $\sim 4\text{%}$ Exon 12).
    • ET: Marked, sustained thrombocytosis with giant, atypical platelets; non-mutated red cell mass; driven by $JAK2$, $CALR$, or $MPL$.
    • PMF: Leukoerythroblastic picture (dacrocytes/teardrops + circulating nRBCs + immature granulocytes) accompanied by marrow reticulin fibrosis (MF-2/MF-3) driven by megakaryocytic PDGF and TGF-$\beta$.
  • MDS Dysplasia Hallmarks: Multi-lineage dysplasia ($\ge 10\text{%}$ within a lineage) features macro-ovalocytes and coarse basophilic stippling in dyserythropoiesis; hypogranular pseudo-Pelger-Huët cells in dysgranulopoiesis; and giant agranular platelets with circulating micromegakaryocytes in dysmegakaryopoiesis.
  • Cytochemical Differential Keys:
    • LAP Score: Differentiates CML (markedly decreased, $< 20$) from benign leukemoid reactions or PV (markedly elevated, $> 160$).
    • Perls' Prussian Blue: Diagnostic criterion for MDS-RS requires $\ge 15\text{%}$ ring sideroblasts ($\ge 5$ granules encircling $\ge 1/3$ of the nucleus), or $\ge 5\text{%}$ in the presence of an $SF3B1$ mutation.
  • Calculations & Bench Interferences:
    • Apply the Corrected WBC calculation whenever nRBCs exceed $10 / 100\text{ WBCs}$.
    • Remediate pseudothrombocytopenia by checking the smear for clumping, redrawing in sodium citrate, and multiplying the automated count by $1.1$.
    • Correct spectrophotometric hemoglobin turbidity in extreme leukocytosis using a plasma blank.
  • Pre-Analytical Tube Rules:
    • EDTA (Purple Top): Standard for automated hematology, smear morphology, and Molecular Diagnostics (PCR/NGS).
    • Sodium Heparin (Green Top): Mandatory for viable Cytogenetic karyotyping; acceptable for Flow Cytometry.
    • Strict Exclusion: Never send heparin for PCR (inhibits Taq polymerase); never send EDTA for karyotyping (inhibits cell division). Keep smears isolated from formalin containers.

ASCP MLS Content Guideline Alignment

Hematology

  • Hematology Disease States
    • Leukocytes (WHO classification)
      • Myeloid neoplasia
        • Myeloproliferative neoplasms (MPN):
          • Chronic Myelogenous Leukemia (CML): Complete myeloid differentiation spectrum on smear (myelocytes, metamyelocytes, bands, segs), basophilia, eosinophilia, low blast percentage ($< 20\text{%}$ in chronic phase); Philadelphia chromosome $t(9;22)(q34.1;q11.2)$ ($BCR::ABL1$ fusion tyrosine kinase).
          • Polycythemia Vera (PV): Clonal panmyelosis, autonomous erythrocytosis, JAK2 V617F mutation, low serum EPO.
          • Essential Thrombocythemia (ET): Sustained thrombocytosis ($\ge 450 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$), giant platelets, JAK2, CALR, or MPL mutations.
          • Primary Myelofibrosis (PMF): Bone marrow fibrosis, dry tap (punctio sicca), massive splenomegaly, leukoerythroblastic smear, teardrop dacrocytes.
        • Myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS): Ineffective hematopoiesis, peripheral cytopenias, dysgranulopoiesis (pseudo-Pelger-Huët, hypogranulation), dyserythropoiesis (ringed sideroblasts, megaloblastoid chromatin), dysmegakaryopoiesis (micromegakaryocytes, unlobulated forms); transformation to acute leukemia.
  • Hematology Laboratory Testing
    • Differentials and Morphology Evaluation (to include blood and body fluids): Leukoerythroblastic picture (circulating nRBCs + immature granulocytes), tear-drop cells (dacrocytes), giant platelets, pseudo-Pelger-Huët neutrophils, circulating blast percentage enumeration.
    • Special Stains
      • Leukocyte Alkaline Phosphatase (LAP): Differentiating CML (severely depressed LAP score $< 20$) from benign Leukemoid Reaction (elevated LAP $> 150$) and Polycythemia Vera (elevated LAP $> 100$).
      • Prussian blue: Identification and quantification of ringed sideroblasts in MDS with ring sideroblasts (SF3B1 mutation).
    • Molecular and Cytogenetic Testing
      • BCR/ABL1 & JAK2: Qualitative and quantitative RT-PCR for $BCR::ABL1$, JAK2 V617F allele discrimination, CALR, MPL; MDS karyotyping ($\text{del}(5q)$, $-7$, $+8$, $\text{del}(20q)$).
