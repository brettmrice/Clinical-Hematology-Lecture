Routine Testing: Automated CBCD Profiling & Smear Morphology

Learning Objectives

  • Differentiate the fundamental pathophysiological mechanisms of Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (MPNs) versus Myelodysplastic Syndromes (MDS), correlating effective clonal hypercellularity with ineffective hematopoiesis, peripheral cytopenias, and morphologic dysplasia.
  • Evaluate Complete Blood Count with Differential (CBCD) parameters, volume histograms, and automated instrument flags to distinguish the profiles of the classic MPNs: Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML), Polycythemia Vera (PV), Essential Thrombocythemia (ET), and Primary Myelofibrosis (PMF).
  • Analyze peripheral blood smear (PBS) morphology to identify hallmark features of MPNs, including the granulocytic left shift, myelocyte bulge, leukoerythroblastosis, and dacrocyte formation.
  • Assess trilineage dysplastic features on peripheral blood smears in suspected MDS, classifying dyserythropoiesis, dysgranulopoiesis (including the pseudo-Pelger-Huët anomaly), and dysmegakaryopoiesis.
  • Apply analytical quality control rules and corrective calculations, including the Rule of Three, nucleated red blood cell (nRBC) white blood cell correction, and pseudothrombocytopenia resolution.

Pathophysiological Divergence: Effective MPN vs. Ineffective MDS

Chronic Myeloid Neoplasms represent clonal hematopoietic stem cell disorders originating from an acquired somatic lesion in an abnormal multipotent progenitor cell. Despite this shared clonal origin, MPNs and MDS diverge along two contrasting pathophysiological trajectories that dictate their clinical, hematological, and morphological presentations:

Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (MPN): Effective Overproduction ("Factory in Overdrive")

MPNs are characterized by autonomous, growth-factor-independent proliferation of one or more myeloid lineages (granulocytic, erythroid, or megakaryocytic). The defining feature is effective hematopoiesis: the abnormal clone produces terminal progeny that mature fully, escape normal bone marrow egress barriers, and flood the systemic circulation.

  • Cellular Dynamics: Bone marrow exhibits marked hypercellularity with expanded myeloid precursors. Apoptosis is normal or inhibited.
  • Peripheral Manifestation: Marked peripheral blood cytosis—extreme leukocytosis (CML), absolute erythrocytosis (PV), or sustained thrombocytosis (ET).
  • Functional Competence: Circulating cells are mature or near-mature, retaining near-normal functional capacity (though platelet function may be paradoxically defective or prothrombotic).

Myelodysplastic Syndromes (MDS): Ineffective Dysplasia ("Defective Factory")

MDS is characterized by clonal stem cell instability leading to disordered differentiation and paradoxical ineffective hematopoiesis.

  • Cellular Dynamics: The bone marrow is frequently hypercellular (in $80\text{--}90\text{%}$ of cases) or normocellular, crowded with proliferating precursors attempting to compensate for peripheral deficits. However, these precursors harbor severe qualitative morphological and genetic defects.
  • Intramedullary Apoptosis: Before these abnormal cells can successfully mature and egress into vascular sinuses, apoptotic death cascades are triggered within the marrow microenvironment. Cells are cleared by bone marrow macrophages before release.
  • Peripheral Manifestation: Despite an active marrow, the patient presents with persistent peripheral blood cytopenias (unilineage cytopenia, bicytopenia, or pancytopenia) accompanied by morphological dysplasia in the surviving cells that enter systemic circulation.

Analytical Quality Control & Mathematical Rule Verification

1. The Rule of Three

For specimens displaying normocytic, normochromic erythrocytes, laboratory data must conform to the Rule of Three:

$$\text{HGB (g/dL)} \times 3 \approx \text{HCT (%)} \pm 3\text{%}$$


$$\text{RBC (}\times 10^6/\mu\text{L)} \times 3 \approx \text{HGB (g/dL)}$$
  • Failure in MPN: In PV, extreme red cell packing can alter optical transmittance or microhematocrit packing. In ET or CML, extreme platelet counts ($> 1,000 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$) or leukocyte counts ($> 100.0 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$) create spectrophotometric turbidity in the hemoglobin lyse cuvette, falsely elevating the HGB reading and producing an impossible $\text{MCHC} > 36\text{ g/dL}$. Centrifuging the sample to perform a plasma blank or saline replacement resolves the interference.
  • Cold Agglutinin Interference vs. True Panmyelosis: Cold agglutinins cross-link erythrocyte I antigens, causing clumping that impedance counters read as single large particles. This yields a spuriously low RBC count, an elevated MCV ($> 110\text{ fL}$), and an impossible MCHC ($> 37\text{ g/dL}$). Pre-warming the EDTA tube to $37^\circ\text{C}$ for 15–30 minutes breaks cold IgM binding, restoring accurate counts. In true PV, RBC, HGB, and HCT are elevated in concert, preserving a normal MCHC ($32\text{--}36\text{ g/dL}$).

2. Nucleated Red Blood Cell (nRBC) WBC Correction

In leukoerythroblastic conditions such as PMF, circulating nucleated red cells resist hypotonic lysis in automated WBC channels and are mistakenly tallied as leukocytes. When an automated count detects or a manual differential confirms $> 10\text{ nRBCs}/100\text{ WBCs}$, the MLS must calculate and report the Corrected WBC count:

$$\text{Corrected WBC (}\times 10^3/\mu\text{L)} = \text{Uncorrected WBC} \times \left( \frac{100}{100 + \text{nRBC}} \right)$$

3. Investigation of Pseudothrombocytopenia

Spurious cytopenias must be ruled out before presuming true marrow failure in suspected MDS:

  • An automated thrombocytopenia flag demands immediate examination of the smear's feathered edge and lateral borders for platelet clumping or platelet satellitism around mature neutrophils (EDTA-dependent agglutination).
  • Corrective Protocol: Redraw the specimen in a 3.2% sodium citrate tube (light blue top). Multiply the automated citrate platelet count by $1.1$ to account for the $9:1$ blood-to-anticoagulant dilution.

4. Smear Preparation Adjustments Based on Hematocrit

  • Polycythemic Blood ($\text{HCT} > 50\text{%}$): Decrease the spreader slide angle ($< 30^\circ$) and push more slowly to prevent an excessively short, thick blood film.
  • Severely Anemic Blood ($\text{HCT} < 35\text{%}$): Increase the spreader slide angle ($> 45^\circ$) and push more rapidly to maintain an adequate monolayer.

1. Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML): The "Marrow-in-the-Blood" Pattern

  • Granulocytic Left Shift Continuum: The smear exhibits the entire continuum of myeloid differentiation: myeloblasts, promyelocytes, myelocytes, metamyelocytes, bands, and segmented neutrophils.
  • The "Myelocyte Bulge": Unlike reactive leukemoid shifts where segmented neutrophils and bands dominate, CML displays a disproportionate peak or "bulge" in the percentage of myelocytes and metamyelocytes, with myelocytes characteristically outnumbering more mature metamyelocytes.
  • Basophilia & Eosinophilia: Absolute basophilia ($> 0.2 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$) is present across all phases. Absolute eosinophilia ($> 0.3 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$) is a frequent companion finding.
  • Blast Enumeration: In the chronic phase of CML, myeloblasts comprise $< 10\text{%}$ of circulating nucleated cells. Myeloblasts are large ($15\text{--}20\text{ }\mu\text{m}$) with fine, lacy chromatin, 1 to 3 distinct nucleoli, and high N:C ratios. Auer rods are absent in classic chronic phase CML.

2. Polycythemia Vera (PV)

  • Erythrocyte Architecture: Pronounced monolayer crowding where normocytic, normochromic erythrocytes touch and overlap. If frequent phlebotomies have depleted iron stores, cells become microcytic and hypochromic ($\text{MCV} < 76\text{ fL}$, $\text{MCH} < 26\text{ pg}$).
  • Leukocytes & Platelets: Neutrophils are mature with normal lobulation; toxic granulation and Döhle bodies are absent. Platelets are quantitatively increased but generally preserve normal morphology.

3. Essential Thrombocythemia (ET)

  • Platelet Aggregation: Massive sheets, clusters, and voluminous aggregates covering multiple high-power fields.
  • Morphological Atypia:
    • Giant Platelets: Measuring $> 4\text{ }\mu\text{m}$, frequently equaling or exceeding the diameter of erythrocytes ($6\text{--}8\text{ }\mu\text{m}$).
    • Hypogranular / Agranular Platelets: Clear, washed-out blue cytoplasm lacking central azurophilic chromomere granules ("ghost platelets").
    • Megakaryocyte Debris: Circulating fragments of megakaryocytic cytoplasm or bare dwarf megakaryocyte nuclei swept to the feathered edge.

4. Primary Myelofibrosis (PMF): The Leukoerythroblastic Triad

As neoplastic megakaryocyte cytokines drive dense fibrous tissue (reticulin and collagen) deposition within marrow sinuses, hematopoiesis relocates to the spleen and liver (extramedullary hematopoiesis), leading to premature cell egress:

  • Dacrocytes (Teardrop Cells): Pathognomonic red cells formed when rigid erythrocytes are stretched and permanently deformed as they squeeze through tight, collagen-choked sinuses or traverse the non-sinusoidal red pulp of an enlarged spleen.
  • Circulating Nucleated Red Blood Cells (nRBCs): Orthochromic and polychromatic normoblasts appear in circulation out of proportion to the degree of anemia.
  • Immature Granulocytic Precursors: Left shift featuring circulating myelocytes, metamyelocytes, and rare promyelocytes.
  • Associated Poikilocytosis: Ovalocytes, rare schistocytes, and polychromatophilic macrocytes.

1. Dysgranulopoiesis (Granulocytic Lineage)

  • Pseudo-Pelger-Huët Anomaly: Acquired failure of nuclear segmentation. Mature neutrophils possess bilobed nuclei connected by a thin, thread-like filament (the "pince-nez" appearance) or entirely unsegmented, round or dumbbell-shaped "pelgeroid" band forms. Nuclear chromatin is hypercondensed and clumped ("pachychromatic").
    • Hereditary vs. Acquired Distinction: In congenital Pelger-Huët anomaly (benign LBR mutation), nearly $100\text{%}$ of neutrophils are hyposegmented with normal cytoplasmic granulation. In acquired pseudo-Pelger-Huët of MDS, hyposegmentation affects a variable fraction of granulocytes, which simultaneously display hypogranulation and co-existing cytopenias.
  • Hypogranulation / Agranularity: Partial or complete failure to synthesize secondary specific granules. Neutrophils appear pale, smooth, or slate-gray.
  • Neutrophil Hypersegmentation: Granulocytes exhibiting $\ge 6$ discrete lobes connected by thin strands, reflecting dysplastic chromatin maturation.
  • Ring-Shaped Nuclei: Neutrophil nuclei possessing a hollow, doughnut-like central configuration.

2. Dyserythropoiesis (Erythroid Lineage)

  • Oval Macrocytes: Erythrocytes are enlarged ($> 8.5\text{ }\mu\text{m}$, $\text{MCV} > 100\text{ fL}$) and distinctly egg-shaped or oval (macro-ovalocytes), differentiating MDS from the round macrocytes observed in non-megaloblastic liver disease or chronic alcoholism.
  • Dimorphic Red Cell Population: Co-existence of two distinct RBC populations: macrocytic normochromic cells and microcytic hypochromic cells. This causes an elevated RDW ($> 14.5\text{%}$) and a bimodal RBC volume histogram.
  • Erythrocyte Inclusions:
    • Coarse Basophilic Stippling: Diffuse, prominent blue-purple granules of precipitated ribosomal RNA resulting from defective heme synthesis. Prussian blue negative.
    • Pappenheimer Bodies (Siderotic Granules): Small, irregular iron-protein aggregates arranged in clusters (typically 2 to 5) at the erythrocyte periphery on Wright-Giemsa stain. Prussian blue positive.
    • Howell-Jolly Bodies: Smooth, round, eccentrically positioned DNA nuclear remnants.
  • Circulating Dysplastic nRBCs: Multinucleated normoblasts, nuclear budding (irregular protrusions), and karyorrhexis (destructive nuclear fragmentation).

3. Dysmegakaryopoiesis (Megakaryocytic Lineage)

  • Giant / Megalocytic Platelets: Platelet diameters exceeding $7\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ with bizarre shapes.
  • Hypogranular / Agranular Platelets: Platelets lacking azurophilic granules, presenting as pale, indistinct discs.
  • Circulating Micromegakaryocytes: Dwarf cells containing a single round, hyper-condensed nucleus surrounded by a small rim of hypogranular platelet cytoplasm.

Differentiation: Peripheral Myeloblasts vs. Immature / Reactive Mononuclear Cells

Quantification of circulating blasts on the 200-cell manual differential provides critical diagnostic and staging data. Under WHO/ICC guidelines, the presence of $\ge 2\text{%}$ peripheral blasts upstages MDS to MDS with Excess Blasts (MDS-EB), while $\ge 20\text{%}$ establishes transformation to secondary Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML).

Reflex Testing, Bone Marrow Cytomorphology & Inter-Laboratory Referral Cascades

Learning Objectives

  • Evaluate bone marrow aspirate cytology and core biopsy histology, contrasting the hypercellularity of effective myeloproliferation with the intramedullary apoptosis and multi-lineage dysplasia of MDS.
  • Perform, score, and clinically interpret the Leukocyte Alkaline Phosphatase (LAP) cytochemical assay, establishing the calculation protocols to differentiate Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML) from benign leukemoid reactions.
  • Apply Perls' Prussian blue cytochemical and Reticulin silver histochemical staining principles to identify and quantify ring sideroblasts and grade marrow myelofibrosis (MF-0 to MF-3).
  • Formulate the specimen handling, pre-analytical criteria, and diagnostic applications for inter-laboratory referrals across Flow Cytometry, Cytogenetics, and Molecular Diagnostics.
  • Appraise the molecular testing hierarchy for chronic myeloid neoplasms, evaluating quantitative RT-qPCR for BCR-ABL1, targeted mutation panels (JAK2, CALR, MPL), and Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS) panels.

Bone Marrow Aspirate & Core Biopsy Evaluation

Definitive classification of chronic myeloid neoplasms requires bone marrow examination combining two complementary specimens obtained via posterior superior iliac crest trephine biopsy:

  1. Bone Marrow Aspirate: Liquid suspension of marrow particles optimized for fine cellular cytology, manual 500-cell differentials, cytochemical stains, flow cytometry, karyotyping, and molecular diagnostics.
  2. Bone Marrow Core Biopsy: Intact cylindrical core of trabecular bone and marrow fixed in $10\text{%}$ neutral buffered formalin, decalcified, and paraffin-embedded. Optimized for assessing overall cellularity, spatial architecture, megakaryocyte topography, and reticulin/collagen fibrosis.

Systematic Examination Parameters

1. Bone Marrow Cellularity

  • Normal Reference: $\text{Cellularity (%)} \approx 100 - \text{Patient Age}\ (\pm 10\text{%})$.
  • MPN Findings: Markedly hypercellular ($80\text{--}100\text{%}$), driven by panmyelosis or unilineage expansion.
  • MDS Findings: Paradoxically hypercellular or normocellular in $80\text{--}90\text{%}$ of cases, confirming ineffective hematopoiesis. The remaining $10\text{--}15\text{%}$ present as hypocellular MDS, which must be distinguished from aplastic anemia.

3. Lineage Maturation & Blast Percentage

  • Myeloid:Erythroid (M:E) Ratio: Normal reference interval is $1.5:1$ to $3:1$ (up to $4:1$). CML frequently drives the M:E ratio $> 10:1$ due to granulocytic proliferation. PV expands erythroid precursors, lowering the M:E ratio toward $1:1$ or reversing it.
  • Aspirate Blast Cutoffs (500-Cell Differential):
    • Normal Baseline: Blasts $< 2\text{%}$ (always $< 5\text{%}$).
    • Lower-Risk MDS / Chronic MPN: Blasts $< 5\text{%}$.
    • MDS with Excess Blasts-1 (MDS-EB-1): $5\text{--}9\text{%}$ blasts in bone marrow (or $2\text{--}4\text{%}$ in peripheral blood).
    • MDS with Excess Blasts-2 (MDS-EB-2): $10\text{--}19\text{%}$ blasts in bone marrow (or $5\text{--}19\text{%}$ in peripheral blood).
    • Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML) Transformation: $\ge 20\text{%}$ myeloblasts in peripheral blood or bone marrow.

4. Investigating the Aspirate "Dry Tap"

Inability to obtain liquid marrow aspirate ("dry tap") occurs in PMF, advanced PV ("spent phase"), or fibrotic MDS. A dry tap indicates mechanical obliteration of marrow sinuses by reticulin/collagen fibrosis or tightly packed cells. The core biopsy is mandatory for diagnosis in these cases.

In-House Manual Cytochemical & Histochemical Procedures

1. Leukocyte Alkaline Phosphatase (LAP) Cytochemistry

The Leukocyte Alkaline Phosphatase (LAP) assay assesses the functional activity of alkaline phosphatase within the secondary (specific) granules of mature granulocytes (segmented neutrophils and bands).

Biochemical Principle

Peripheral blood smears are incubated with a naphthol AS-BI phosphate substrate at an alkaline pH ($\approx 9.5$). Active alkaline phosphatase in neutrophil secondary granules hydrolyzes the ester:

$$\text{Naphthol AS-BI Phosphate} \xrightarrow{\text{Alkaline Phosphatase}} \text{Naphthol AS-BI} + \text{Phosphate}$$


The liberated naphthol AS-BI couples with a diazonium salt (Fast Red Violet LB), precipitating an insoluble, visible red azo dye at the intracellular site of enzyme activity. Smears are counterstained with hematoxylin.

Detailed Laboratory Protocol & Specimen Requirements

  • Specimen Collection: Fresh capillary blood or blood anticoagulated with sodium heparin (green top).
  • Strict Contraindication: EDTA is strictly prohibited. EDTA chelates $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ and $\text{Zn}^{2+}$, which are essential divalent metallic cofactors for alkaline phosphatase, causing false-negative (depressed) scores.
  • Fixation & Processing: Prepare smears within 1 hour of draw. Air-dry and fix immediately in cold ($0\text{--}4^\circ\text{C}$) buffered formalin-methanol solution for 30 seconds. Enzyme activity deteriorates rapidly if smears remain unfixed at room temperature.
  • Incubation: Incubate in naphthol AS-BI / Fast Red Violet solution at $37^\circ\text{C}$ for 15 minutes. Rinse with deionized water. Counterstain with filtered hematoxylin for 1–2 minutes, wash, and air dry.

Scoring System & Mathematical Calculation

Under $100\times$ oil immersion, 100 consecutive mature neutrophils (segmented and bands) are enumerated and assigned an intensity score from 0 to 4+:

Score Granule Precipitation Density Visual Microscopic Appearance
0 Negative Clear, colorless cytoplasm; no precipitate
1+ Trace / Faint Scattered, fine pink-red granules filling $< 25\text{%}$ of cytoplasmic volume
2+ Moderate Moderate precipitate filling $25\text{--}50\text{%}$ of cytoplasmic volume
3+ Strong / Dense Dense, coarse granules filling $50\text{--}80\text{%}$ of cytoplasm; nuclear outline preserved
4+ Confluent / Severe Solid, deeply confluent precipitate filling $100\text{%}$ of cytoplasm; nucleus partially obscured

$$\text{LAP Score} = \sum_{i=0}^{4} \left( \text{Score Category } i \times \text{Number of Cells in Category } i \right)$$


Theoretical score range: 0 to 400.

Clinical Interpretation & Quality Control

  • Reference Range: Normal LAP score interval is 20 to 130.
  • Decreased LAP Score ($< 20$): Characteristic of Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML), where clonal neutrophils lack functional alkaline phosphatase, and Paroxysmal Nocturnal Hemoglobinuria (PNH).
  • Elevated LAP Score ($> 130 - 160$): Characteristic of Leukemoid Reactions (severe sepsis, infection, tissue necrosis), Polycythemia Vera (PV), third-trimester pregnancy, and glucocorticoid therapy.
  • Quality Control Slide Requirements: Every analytical run must include a normal control slide alongside an elevated control slide (obtained from a third-trimester pregnant donor or confirmed reactive leukocytosis). If the positive control fails to show 3+ or 4+ positivity, patient results are invalid.

2. Perls' Prussian Blue Cytochemical Iron Stain

Perls' Prussian blue detects non-heme ferric iron ($\text{Fe}^{3+}$) within marrow macrophage storage depots and red cell precursors.

Chemical Principle

Aspirate smears are treated with a freshly prepared mixture of equal parts aqueous potassium ferrocyanide and dilute hydrochloric acid (HCl):

$$4\text{FeCl}_3 + 3\text{K}_4[\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_6] \xrightarrow{\text{HCl}} \text{Fe}_4[\text{Fe}(\text{CN})_6]_3\ (\text{Insoluble Prussian Blue Pigment}) + 12\text{KCl}$$


Dilute HCl releases $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ from ferritin and hemosiderin. Ferric ions react with potassium ferrocyanide to form insoluble ferric ferrocyanide (Prussian blue), an intense blue precipitate. Heme-bound iron within protoporphyrin is unreactive. Smears are counterstained with Nuclear Fast Red.

Morphological Evaluation & Ring Sideroblast Diagnostic Thresholds

  • Storage Iron Evaluation: Scored in marrow spicules under $10\times$ from 0 (depleted) to 4+ (massive hemosiderin aggregates). Iron is absent in iron deficiency anemia, but normal to increased in MDS.
  • Normal Sideroblasts: $20\text{--}50\text{%}$ of normoblasts contain 1 to 4 faint, randomly scattered cytoplasmic blue specks.
  • Ring Sideroblasts: Pathognomonic dysplastic erythroid precursors defined as normoblasts containing $\ge 5$ iron granules encircling at least one-third ($\ge 33\text{%}$) of the nucleus. This forms when mitochondrial ferrochelatase or heme biosynthesis fails, trapping iron within mitochondria positioned around the nuclear rim.
  • Diagnostic Criteria for MDS with Ring Sideroblasts (MDS-RS):
    • $\ge 15\text{%}$ ring sideroblasts among marrow erythroid precursors; OR
    • $\ge 5\text{%}$ ring sideroblasts if a concurrent clonal SF3B1 spliceosome gene mutation is confirmed.

3. Reticulin Silver Histochemical Staining (Core Biopsy)

Reticulin silver impregnation (Gomori or Gordon-Sweets) is performed exclusively on thin ($3\text{--}4\text{ }\mu\text{m}$) sections of formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded core biopsies to assess marrow fibrosis. Reticulin fibers cannot be evaluated on liquid aspirate smears.

Histochemical Principle

Reticulin is composed of thin, branching Type III collagen fibers. Sections are oxidized with potassium permanganate, sensitized with iron alum, and exposed to an ammoniacal silver solution:

$$\text{Reticulin Fibers} \xrightarrow{\text{Ammoniacal Silver}} \text{Silver Protein Complexes} \xrightarrow{\text{Formalin Reduction}} \text{Metallic Silver (Black Deposits)}$$


Unreduced silver is removed with sodium thiosulfate. Fibers appear as crisp, black branching lines against a light pink counterstained background.

Pathophysiological Mechanism of Fibrogenesis

Reticulin deposition is a reactive, secondary response mediated by polyclonal marrow fibroblasts. Clonal megakaryocytes in PMF overproduce and leak fibrogenic cytokines—principally Platelet-Derived Growth Factor (PDGF) and Transforming Growth Factor-beta (TGF-$\beta$)—into the extracellular matrix, stimulating collagen deposition.

European Consensus Grading System for Myelofibrosis

  • Grade MF-0 (Normal): Scattered linear reticulin fibers; no intersections.
  • Grade MF-1 (Mild / Early Fibrosis): Loose network of thin, branching reticulin fibers, localized primarily around perivascular spaces.
  • Grade MF-2 (Moderate / Overt Fibrosis): Diffuse, dense reticulin network with extensive intersections and focal coarse collagen bundles (Masson trichrome positive). Meets the major diagnostic criterion for overt PMF.
  • Grade MF-3 (Severe / Advanced Fibrosis): Diffuse, dense reticulin network with coarse collagen bundles associated with osteosclerosis (new bone formation).

1. Flow Cytometry (Immunophenotyping) Referral

  • Specimen Requirements: Bone marrow aspirate or peripheral blood collected in sodium heparin (green top) or EDTA (lavender top). Specimen must contain unfixed, viable cells maintained at ambient temperature ($20\text{--}25^\circ\text{C}$). Clotted specimens must be rejected.
  • Gating & Analytical Endpoints: CD45 versus side scatter (SSC) gating isolates progenitor myeloblasts (dim CD45, low SSC).
    • Enumerate CD34+ and CD117+ progenitor populations.
    • Detect phenotypic aberrancies characterizing MDS: aberrant lineage infidelity (e.g., expression of T-cell marker CD7 or neural marker CD56 on myeloid blasts), asynchronous CD13/CD33/CD11b co-expression, or abnormal erythroid CD71/CD36 patterns.

2. Conventional Cytogenetics (Karyotyping) & FISH Referral

  • Specimen Requirements: 2 to 3 mL of viable bone marrow aspirate collected in a sodium heparin (green top) tube. Transport at ambient temperature ($18\text{--}25^\circ\text{C}$). Do not freeze, do not fix in formalin, and do not collect in EDTA (EDTA suppresses viable mitogen-driven mitotic growth).
  • Laboratory Protocol:
    1. Culture: Mitogen stimulation to induce mitotic division.
    2. Metaphase Arrest: Incubation with colcemid to disrupt spindle fiber microtubules, arresting cells in metaphase.
    3. Harvest: Hypotonic swelling ($0.075\text{ M KCl}$) followed by Carnoy's fixation (3:1 methanol:glacial acetic acid).
    4. G-Banding & Analysis: Trypsin digestion and Giemsa staining; analysis of a minimum of 20 metaphase spreads.

  • Hallmark Karyotypic Findings:

    • CML: The Philadelphia chromosome, der(22), produced by the reciprocal translocation:

      $$\text{t}(9;22)(\text{q}34.1;\text{q}11.2)$$


      Present in $\approx 95\text{%}$ of CML cases. Fluorescence In Situ Hybridization (FISH) confirms BCR-ABL1 probe fusion signals in interphase nuclei.

    • MDS: Diagnostic and prognostic lesions driving the Revised International Prognostic Scoring System (IPSS-R):

    • Favorable: Isolated deletion 5q [$\text{del}(5\text{q})$], isolated $\text{del}(20\text{q})$, or normal karyotype.
    • Adverse / High Risk: Monosomy 7 ($-7$), deletion 7q [$\text{del}(7\text{q})$], or complex karyotypes ($\ge 3$ distinct abnormalities).
    • Intermediate: Trisomy 8 ($+8$).

3. Molecular Diagnostics: Targeted Driver Mutations & NGS Panels

Molecular assays confirm clonality, detect cytogenetically cryptic alterations, and establish baseline targets for therapeutic monitoring.

  • Specimen Requirements: Peripheral blood or bone marrow aspirate collected in EDTA (lavender top). Heparin is strictly contraindicated as heparin inhibits Taq DNA polymerase enzymes during downstream PCR amplification. For RNA testing (BCR-ABL1 RT-qPCR), specimens must be processed within 24–48 hours to avoid endogenous RNase degradation.

CML: Quantitative RT-qPCR for BCR-ABL1

  • Principle: Total cellular RNA is extracted and reverse-transcribed into complementary DNA (cDNA). Real-time quantitative PCR (TaqMan qPCR) amplifies the junction of the chimeric BCR-ABL1 mRNA transcript alongside an internal reference gene (ABL1 or GUSB).
  • Monitoring: Tracks minimal residual disease (MRD) during tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI; e.g., imatinib) therapy. Measures achievement of Major Molecular Response (MMR), defined as a $\ge 3$-log reduction ($\le 0.1\text{%}$ or $\le 0.001$) on the International Scale (IS).

  • JAK2 V617F: Valine-to-phenylalanine substitution at codon 617 within the autoinhibitory pseudokinase domain of JAK2, conferring constitutive kinase activity. Present in $> 95\text{%}$ of PV and $50\text{--}60\text{%}$ of ET and PMF.

MDS Targeted Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS) Panels

Because conventional karyotyping is normal in $\approx 50\text{%}$ of MDS patients, NGS targeted gene panels provide molecular evidence of clonality and refine prognostic risk:

  • SF3B1: Splicing factor mutation strongly correlated with ring sideroblasts on Prussian blue staining and associated with a favorable clinical outcome.
  • TP53: Tumor suppressor mutation associated with multi-complex cytogenetics, resistance to hypomethylating agents, rapid leukemic transformation, and poor overall survival.
  • Epigenetic / Splicing Regulators: Mutations in TET2, ASXL1, DNMT3A, RUNX1, and SRSF2. High-risk mutations (ASXL1, SRSF2, RUNX1) predict rapid progression to secondary AML.

ASCP MLS Content Guideline Alignment

Clinical Hematology

  • Hematopoietic Pathophysiology: Autonomous stem cell proliferation and effective maturation (MPN) versus disordered differentiation, intramedullary apoptosis, and peripheral cytopenias (MDS).
  • Automated Complete Blood Count (CBC) & Indices:
  • Diagnostic evaluation of WBC, RBC, HGB, HCT, MCV, MCH, MCHC, and RDW across CML, PV, ET, PMF, and MDS.
  • Application and calculation of the Rule of Three: $\text{HGB} \times 3 \approx \text{HCT} \pm 3\text{%}$.
  • Application and calculation of the Corrected WBC count for circulating nRBCs: $\text{Corrected WBC} = \text{Uncorrected WBC} \times [100 / (100 + \text{nRBC})]$.
  • Calculation of absolute leukocyte subsets: $\text{Absolute Count} = \text{Total WBC} \times (\text{Relative %} / 100)$.
  • Erythrocyte Morphology & Inclusions:
  • Identification and clinical significance of dacrocytes (teardrop cells) in Primary Myelofibrosis.
  • Identification of dyserythropoiesis: oval macrocytes, dimorphic erythrocyte populations, coarse basophilic stippling, and dysplastic circulating nucleated red blood cells.
  • Differentiation of intraerythrocytic inclusions: Pappenheimer bodies (siderotic iron granules; Prussian blue positive), basophilic stippling (RNA; Prussian blue negative), and Howell-Jolly bodies (DNA remnants).
  • Leukocyte Morphology & Alterations:
  • Recognition of the complete granulocytic maturation continuum, the "myelocyte bulge," absolute basophilia, and absolute eosinophilia in CML.
  • Differentiation of dysgranulopoiesis: acquired pseudo-Pelger-Huët anomaly (hyposegmented "pince-nez" neutrophils with condensed chromatin) and cytoplasmic hypogranulation/agranularity.
  • Microscopic blast differentiation criteria (myeloblast vs. reactive lymphocyte vs. monocyte) and blast staging thresholds for MDS-EB-1 ($5\text{--}9\text{%}$), MDS-EB-2 ($10\text{--}19\text{%}$), and acute leukemic transformation ($\ge 20\text{%}$).
  • Platelet Morphology & Megakaryocytic Alterations:
  • Qualitative and quantitative platelet defects: marked thrombocytosis, giant platelets, agranular "ghost" platelets, and circulating megakaryocytic fragments.
  • Bone marrow core biopsy megakaryocyte topography: giant "staghorn" hyperlobated megakaryocytes in ET/PMF versus hypolobated micromegakaryocytes in MDS.
  • Cytochemical & Histochemical Procedures:
  • Leukocyte Alkaline Phosphatase (LAP) cytochemical assay: biochemical principle, Fast Red Violet LB coupling, 0 to 4+ scoring criteria, mathematical score calculation, and clinical differentiation of CML ($< 20$) from leukemoid reactions and PV ($> 130$).
  • Perls' Prussian blue reaction: chemical mechanism of ferric ferrocyanide precipitation, evaluation of macrophage storage iron, ring sideroblast morphology ($\ge 5$ granules encircling $\ge 33\text{%}$ of the nucleus), and diagnostic criteria for MDS-RS.
  • Reticulin silver staining: ammoniacal silver impregnation of Type III collagen fibers, cytokine-mediated fibrogenesis mechanisms (PDGF/TGF-$\beta$), and European Consensus grading (MF-0 to MF-3) for Primary Myelofibrosis.

Molecular Diagnostics & Cytogenetics

  • Conventional Cytogenetics & FISH:
  • Specimen handling (sodium heparin bone marrow aspirate), viable cell culture, colcemid metaphase arrest, hypotonic treatment, G-banding, and analysis of $\ge 20$ metaphase spreads.
  • Identification and diagnostic confirmation of the Philadelphia chromosome: $\text{t}(9;22)(\text{q}34.1;\text{q}11.2)$.
  • Prognostic cytogenetic aberrations in MDS within the IPSS-R framework: $\text{del}(5\text{q})$, $-7/\text{del}(7\text{q})$, $+8$, and $\text{del}(20\text{q})$.
  • Molecular Diagnostic Testing & Methods:
  • Quantitative RT-qPCR for BCR-ABL1 fusion transcripts and monitoring of Major Molecular Response (MMR) on the International Scale (IS).
  • Targeted driver mutation assays for Philadelphia-negative MPNs: JAK2 V617F (exon 14 point mutation), JAK2 exon 12, CALR exon 9 indels, and MPL exon 10 mutations (W515L/K).
  • Mutational exclusivity patterns and diagnostic algorithms for PV, ET, PMF, and triple-negative MPN.
  • Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS) panels for MDS: clinical significance of SF3B1 mutations (correlation with ring sideroblasts and favorable prognosis), TP53 mutations (correlation with complex karyotypes and poor prognosis), and epigenetic regulators (TET2, ASXL1, DNMT3A, RUNX1).

Laboratory Operations & Quality Control

  • Pre-Analytical Specimen Triage & Anticoagulant Integrity:
  • Anticoagulant selection: EDTA for routine CBCD, blood smears, and molecular DNA/RNA extraction; Sodium Heparin for Cytogenetics, Flow Cytometry, and LAP cytochemistry.
  • Pre-analytical contraindications: EDTA chelation of divalent cations causing false-negative LAP scores; heparin inhibition of Taq DNA polymerase in PCR testing; formalin fixation or freezing causing mitotic failure in cytogenetics.
  • Recognition and remediation of EDTA-dependent pseudothrombocytopenia using 3.2% sodium citrate tubes and the $1.1\times$ dilution correction factor.
  • Detection and corrective warming protocols ($37^\circ\text{C}$ for 15–30 minutes) for cold agglutinin red cell agglutination.
  • Remediation of spectrophotometric hemoglobin turbidity in extreme leukocytosis and thrombocytosis via plasma blank or saline replacement.
  • Preparation technique modifications (angle and speed adjustments) for polycythemic versus severely anemic blood smears.
  • Inter-Laboratory Reflex Algorithms:
  • Systematic triage routing automated analyzer flags (IG, nRBC, extreme cytosis, refractory cytopenias) to manual smear review, bone marrow biopsy, flow cytometry, cytogenetics, and molecular diagnostics.
