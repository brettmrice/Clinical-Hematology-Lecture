Routine Hematology Testing: Automated Indices, Smear Morphology, and Blast Discrimination

Learning Objectives

  • Differentiate precursor lymphoid neoplasms from mature lymphoproliferative disorders and acute myeloid leukemias using complete blood count parameters, automated analyzer flags, and peripheral blood smear cytomorphology.
  • Identify and contrast pathognomonic peripheral blood smear morphology across mature B-cell, T-cell, and NK-cell neoplasms, including CLL, B-PLL, HCL, Burkitt lymphoma, Waldenström macroglobulinemia, MCL, FL, T-LGL, and Sézary syndrome.
  • Evaluate the pathophysiological mechanisms of paraprotein-induced analytical interferences on automated hematology analyzers, identifying spurious hemoglobin and MCHC elevations and executing saline replacement protocols.
  • Distinguish true antigen-antibody erythrocyte agglutination from paraprotein-mediated rouleaux formation using physiological principles and benchtop laboratory procedures.
  • Apply pre-analytical quality control protocols to correct for smear-based artifacts, including the 22% bovine serum albumin slide preparation for smudge cell stabilization and white blood cell count corrections for nucleated red blood cells.
  • Distinguish primary circulating leukemic processes from secondary, cytokine-driven reactive hematologic responses observed in Classic Hodgkin Lymphoma.

Precursor vs. Mature Lymphoid Differentiation & The Blast Morphologic Challenge

Lymphoid neoplasms arise from the clonal expansion of cells arrested at specific stages of lymphoid ontogeny. The primary division separates precursor neoplasms (acute lymphoblastic leukemia/lymphoblastic lymphoma [ALL/LBL]) from mature, post-thymic or post-germinal center peripheral lymphoproliferative disorders (e.g., chronic lymphocytic leukemia [CLL], prolymphocytic leukemias, and non-Hodgkin lymphomas).

Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia/Lymphoma (ALL) Hemogram

ALL represents the clonal proliferation of immature B- or T-lymphoblasts within the bone marrow, rapidly compromising normal hematopoiesis:

  • Erythroid Line: Normocytic, normochromic anemia secondary to bone marrow replacement (erythroid hypoplasia). Hemoglobin is frequently $< 8.0\text{ g/dL}$ (standard reference range: $12.0\text{ -- }18.0\text{ g/dL}$).
  • Thrombocytic Line: Severe thrombocytopenia (frequently $< 50 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$; standard reference range: $150\text{ -- }450 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$) due to megakaryocyte displacement, resulting in petechiae, purpura, and mucosal hemorrhage.
  • Leukocyte Line: Total WBC count is variable: it can be severely depressed (aleukemic leukemia, $< 3.6 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$), normal, or markedly elevated ($> 100.0 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$). Absolute neutropenia is common (ANC frequently $< 1.0 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$; standard reference range: $1.7\text{ -- }7.5 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$), predisposing patients to opportunistic infections.
  • Automated Analyzer Flags: Modern multi-angle optical scatter and fluorescence instruments generate flags such as "Blasts/Abn Lympho", "Atypical Lymphs", or "Suspect Population", identifying events within the low-side-scatter, high-fluorescence/volume differential channels.

Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia / Small Lymphocytic Lymphoma (CLL/SLL)

  • CBC Profile: Persistent, absolute mature lymphocytosis $> 5.0 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$ (frequently $20.0\text{ -- }150.0 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$; lymphocyte relative differential often $> 80\text{%}$). Hemoglobin and platelets are generally preserved until advanced stages.
  • Smear Morphology: Monotonous field of small, mature-appearing lymphocytes ($7\text{ -- }10\text{ }\mu\text{m}$, comparable to normal resting erythrocytes). The nucleus contains dense, clumped heterochromatin blocks separated by distinct light parachromatin spaces, classically termed "soccer-ball", "turtle-shell", or "cracked-mud" chromatin. Nucleoli are absent.
  • Smudge Cells: Stripped, disrupted nuclear chromatin aggregates ("basket cells" or "Gumprecht shadows") generated by mechanical shear during slide spreading.

B-Cell Prolymphocytic Leukemia (B-PLL)

  • CBC Profile: Marked leukocytosis, characteristically $> 100.0 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$, rapidly progressing with normocytic anemia and thrombocytopenia.
  • Smear Morphology: Prolymphocytes constitute $> 55\text{%}$ of all circulating lymphoid cells. Cells are medium-to-large ($15\text{ -- }18\text{ }\mu\text{m}$) with moderate, faintly basophilic cytoplasm. The round, central nucleus contains condensed chromatin and a single, prominent, central, vesicular nucleolus.

Hairy Cell Leukemia (HCL)

  • CBC Profile: Pancytopenia across all three cell lines. Marked, persistent absolute monocytopenia ($< 0.1 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$, frequently $0.0 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$; reference range: $0.1\text{ -- }1.3 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$) is an important diagnostic indicator.
  • Smear Morphology: Cells are medium-to-large ($15\text{ -- }20\text{ }\mu\text{m}$) with abundant, pale, grayish-blue, transparent cytoplasm. The cytoplasmic borders demonstrate circumferential, delicate, thread-like "hair-like" projections or ruffled margins. The nucleus is eccentric, oval, or reniform (kidney bean-shaped), displaying open, spongy, net-like chromatin without distinct nucleoli.

Burkitt Lymphoma / Leukemia (Mature B-ALL)

  • CBC Profile: Rapidly developing leukocytosis with cytopenias; automated analyzers flag extensive blasts.
  • Smear Morphology: Uniform population of medium-sized blastoid cells. Cytoplasm is intensely basophilic ("royal blue" or "midnight blue"). The hallmark feature is the presence of numerous sharply demarcated, unstained, punched-out lipid vacuoles overlying both the cytoplasm and the nucleus. The nucleus is round to oval with coarse granular chromatin and $2\text{ -- }5$ distinct nucleoli.
  • Diagnostic Disturbance: The histological "starry sky" appearance is caused by tingible-body macrophages phagocytosing apoptotic debris within fixed tissue sections (lymph node or marrow core) and is never observed on a peripheral blood smear.

Waldenström Macroglobulinemia (Lymphoplasmacytic Lymphoma - LPL)

  • CBC Profile: Normocytic, normochromic anemia accentuated by hemodilution from expanded plasma volume. Spurious parameter flags arise from hyperviscous monoclonal IgM.
  • Smear Morphology: Striking red cell rouleaux against a diffuse, pale blue-gray proteinaceous background. The lymphoid lineage demonstrates a continuous maturation spectrum: small, mature lymphocytes, intermediate plasmacytoid lymphocytes (cells with eccentric round nuclei, condensed chromatin, and basophilic cytoplasm), and mature plasma cells.

Mantle Cell Lymphoma (MCL) & Follicular Lymphoma (FL)

  • MCL Morphology: Monotonous population of small-to-medium lymphocytes with scant pale cytoplasm and irregular, deeply indented, notched, or folded nuclear contours.
  • FL Morphology (Centrocytes): Circulating cleaved follicular center cells ("butt cells" or "buttockoid cells") displaying an elongated nucleus with a deep, narrow cleavage or cleft running through the center, containing dense, coarse chromatin.
  • Differentiation from Reactive Lymphocytes: Reactive lymphocytes (e.g., in infectious mononucleosis) are morphologically pleomorphic, variable in size and shape, exhibit low N:C ratios, and have abundant cytoplasm that flares and indents around adjacent erythrocytes. Malignant cells in MCL and FL are morphologically uniform and monotonous.

Mature T-Cell and NK-Cell Neoplasms

  • T-Cell Large Granular Lymphocytic (T-LGL) Leukemia:
    • CBC Profile: Chronic, severe absolute neutropenia (ANC frequently $< 0.5 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$) with modest absolute lymphocytosis ($2.0\text{ -- }10.0 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$). Pure Red Cell Aplasia (PRCA) is observed in a subset of patients.
    • Smear Morphology: Monotonous population of large lymphocytes ($12\text{ -- }15\text{ }\mu\text{m}$) with abundant pale blue, transparent cytoplasm containing prominent, coarse, azurophilic (magenta-purple) cytoplasmic granules clustered near the centrosome.
  • Sézary Syndrome (Cutaneous T-Cell Lymphoma - CTCL):
    • CBC Profile: Leukocytosis with absolute lymphocytosis and peripheral eosinophilia (absolute eosinophil count $> 0.3 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$; reference range: $0\text{ -- }0.3 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$) accompanying generalized erythroderma.
    • Smear Morphology: Circulating Sézary cells are small-to-large atypical lymphocytes dominated by a hyperconvoluted, grooved, folded cerebriform nucleus resembling the sulci and gyri of the brain. The chromatin is dense and hyperchromatic.
  • T-Cell Prolymphocytic Leukemia (T-PLL):
    • CBC Profile: Rapidly progressive, extreme leukocytosis ($> 100.0 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$) with thrombocytopenia and anemia.
    • Smear Morphology: Medium-sized prolymphocytes with a prominent vesicular nucleolus. Unlike B-PLL, T-PLL cells exhibit irregular, convoluted, notched, or folded nuclear contours and moderately basophilic cytoplasm that may form small cytoplasmic blebs or protrusions.

Classic Hodgkin Lymphoma is a localized malignancy of altered germinal center B-cells characterized by multinucleated Reed-Sternberg (RS) cells residing within affected lymph nodes.

  • Secondary Reactive Peripheral Profile: The peripheral hemogram reflects reactive bone marrow alterations in response to tumor-secreted cytokines:

Pathophysiology of Paraprotein Analytical Interference

Monoclonal immunoglobulins interact with automated complete blood count analyzers, causing spurious results across spectrophotometric and electrical impedance channels:

  1. Spectrophotometric Interference: During automated hemoglobin measurement, erythrocyte membranes are lysed, and released hemoglobin is converted to a stable chromogen (e.g., cyanmethemoglobin or SLS-hemoglobin) measured photometrically at $540\text{ nm}$. Paraproteins precipitate when exposed to low-ionic-strength, acidic lyse reagents, producing solution turbidity. This increases light absorbance, resulting in a spuriously elevated hemoglobin measurement.

  2. Calculated Indices Discordance: Because the automated hematocrit (derived from RBC count and MCV) is typically unaffected by paraprotein precipitation, calculated indices that rely on hemoglobin are distorted:

    $$\text{MCHC (g/dL)} = \frac{\text{HGB (g/dL)} \times 100}{\text{HCT (%)}}$$


    The falsely elevated hemoglobin causes the MCHC to exceed the physiological limit of red blood cell packaging ($> 36.0\text{ g/dL}$, frequently reaching $38.0\text{ -- }45.0\text{ g/dL}$).

  3. Failure of the Rule of Three: In specimens with normocytic, normochromic erythrocytes, the hematocrit should approximate three times the hemoglobin value:

    $$\text{HGB (g/dL)} \times 3 \approx \text{HCT (%)} \pm 3\text{%}$$


    Paraprotein precipitation disrupts this relationship, causing $\text{HGB} \times 3$ to exceed the measured hematocrit.

Morphology of Plasma Cell Dyscrasias

  • Rouleaux Formation: Erythrocytes align face-to-face in organized linear chains resembling stacks of coins.
    • Biophysical Mechanism: Normal erythrocytes maintain separation via mutual electrostatic repulsion (zeta potential), established by negatively charged sialic acid residues on membrane glycophorins. Basic, positively charged circulating paraproteins coat the erythrocyte membrane, neutralizing the negative charge and allowing red blood cells to adhere.
  • Diffuse Background Staining: Wright-Giemsa dyes react with the elevated protein content in the plasma, creating a hazy, diffuse blue-gray background discoloration across the blood film.
  • Circulating Plasma Cells: Ovoid cells ($12\text{ -- }20\text{ }\mu\text{m}$) with abundant, deeply basophilic cytoplasm. The eccentric, round nucleus displays clumped heterochromatin arranged in a "clock-face" or "cartwheel" configuration. A prominent, unstained pale crescent adjacent to the nucleus represents the hypertrophied Golgi apparatus (perinuclear hof). If circulating plasma cells exceed $20\text{%}$ of total leukocytes or reach an absolute count $\ge 2.0 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$, the condition is classified as Plasma Cell Leukemia.

Corrected WBC Calculation for Nucleated Red Blood Cells (nRBCs)

In advanced lymphoid leukemias or leucoerythroblastic reactions, bone marrow architectural displacement releases immature nucleated red blood cells into the peripheral blood. Automated impedance analyzers count nRBCs as leukocytes due to their resistant unlysed nuclei:

  • Mathematical Calculation:

    $$\text{Corrected WBC } (\times 10^3/\mu\text{L}) = \text{Uncorrected WBC } (\times 10^3/\mu\text{L}) \times \left(\frac{100}{100 + \text{nRBC}}\right)$$


    (where $\text{nRBC}$ is expressed as the number of nucleated erythrocytes per $100\text{ WBCs}$).

  • QC Rule: If manual peripheral smear examination reveals $\ge 10\text{ nRBCs/100 WBCs}$, the technologist must report the corrected WBC count and recalculate all absolute leukocyte differentials.

Core Biopsy Histological Infiltration Patterns

  • Interstitial Infiltration: Neoplastic lymphoid cells infiltrate between preserved adipocytes and residual normal hematopoietic colonies without effacing native microarchitecture. Characteristic of early-stage CLL, Hairy Cell Leukemia, and T-LGL leukemia.
  • Nodular (Focal) Infiltration: Malignant cells form rounded, well-circumscribed aggregates within intertrabecular marrow spaces, sparing adjacent architecture.
  • Paratrabecular Infiltration: Neoplastic lymphoid aggregates directly line and cap the bony trabeculae. This pattern is pathognomonic for Follicular Lymphoma and reflects specific adhesion molecule expression.
  • Diffuse Infiltration: Monolithic replacement of the marrow space by dense sheets of neoplastic lymphoid cells, effacing fat spaces and eradicating normal myeloid, erythroid, and megakaryocytic elements. Characteristic of acute lymphoblastic leukemia, Burkitt lymphoma, and advanced diffuse large B-cell lymphoma.

Bone Marrow Aspiration Failure: The "Dry Tap" Protocol

Aspiration failure (punctio sicca or "dry tap") occurs when marrow fluid cannot be drawn into the aspiration syringe.

  • Pathophysiological Mechanisms:
    1. Reticulin and Collagen Fibrosis: Neoplastic cells (notably in Hairy Cell Leukemia) produce fibrogenic cytokines (e.g., TGF-$\beta$, bFGF) that induce marrow fibroblasts to synthesize extensive extracellular reticulin networks that trap hematopoietic cells.
    2. Monolithic Cell Density: Tightly packed neoplastic cells (in acute leukemias or diffuse large cell lymphomas) aggregate with insufficient interstitial fluid to permit fluid aspiration.
  • Bench Remediation Procedure: When an aspirate cannot be obtained, fluid-dependent tests (routine flow cytometry, karyotyping) are compromised. The technologist must immediately prepare multiple touch preparations (imprints) by gently rolling the unfixed core biopsy across clean glass slides before formalin immersion. Touch preparations provide uncrushed, air-dried single-cell films for Wright-Giemsa evaluation, cytochemistry, and rapid interphase FISH.

Myeloperoxidase (MPO)

  • Biochemical Principle: Myeloperoxidase resides inside the primary azurophilic granules of myeloid lineage cells. In the presence of hydrogen peroxide ($\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$), intracellular MPO catalyzes the oxidation of a chromogenic substrate (e.g., 3,3'-diaminobenzidine), forming an insoluble brown-black precipitate.
  • Diagnostic Interpretation: MPO is an exclusion marker in lymphoid hematology. Lymphoblasts are strictly MPO-negative. Demonstration of $\ge 3\text{%}$ MPO-positive blasts definitively confirms Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML) and excludes ALL.
  • QC Consideration: Minimally differentiated AML (AML-M0) lacks cytochemically detectable MPO activity, requiring flow cytometric confirmation of cytoplasmic MPO (cMPO). Enzyme activity degrades in unpreserved slides stored $> 1\text{ week}$.

Terminal deoxynucleotidyl Transferase (TdT)

  • Biochemical Principle: TdT is an intranuclear, template-independent DNA polymerase that adds deoxynucleoside triphosphates to the 3'-hydroxyl termini of oligonucleotide primers during somatic V(D)J recombination.
  • Diagnostic Interpretation: Primary marker of lymphoid immaturity. Demonstrates intense nuclear localization in $> 95\text{%}$ of precursor B-ALL and precursor T-ALL cases. Mature B- and T-cell neoplasms are strictly TdT-negative.
  • QC Consideration: TdT expression may occur aberrantly in a small subset ($5\text{ -- }10\text{%}$) of AML cases, particularly those with core-binding factor translocations or $t(9;22)$.

Tartrate-Resistant Acid Phosphatase (TRAP)

  • Biochemical Principle: Acid phosphatases are lysosomal enzymes found in multiple cell types that hydrolyze monophosphates under acidic conditions. Seven isoenzymes ($0\text{ through }6$) exist. Standard isoenzymes are inhibited by $\text{L}(+)$-tartaric acid. Neoplastic hairy cells produce high concentrations of isoenzyme 5, which is resistant to tartrate inhibition.
  • Diagnostic Interpretation: Cytochemical confirmation of Hairy Cell Leukemia, characterized by intense crimson-red granular cytoplasmic precipitation in the presence of tartrate.
  • QC Consideration: TRAP positivity may occasionally be observed in prolymphocytic leukemia and Sézary syndrome. Flow cytometric immunophenotyping is the modern standard of care.

Analytical Principles and Gating

  • Hydrodynamic Focusing: Single-cell suspensions flow through a central core fluid bordered by sheath fluid, aligning cells individually across focused laser beams.
  • Light Scatter:
    • Forward Scatter (FSC): Measures diffracted light along the path of the laser, proportional to cell surface area and volume.
    • Side Scatter (SSC): Measures refracted and reflected light at a 90° angle, reflecting internal cellular complexity, nuclear lobulation, and cytoplasmic granularity.
  • CD45 vs. Side Scatter Gating Strategy: Plotting CD45 (Leukocyte Common Antigen) intensity against SSC separates leukocytes into distinct populations:
    • Lymphoblasts: Characterized by dim/intermediate CD45 expression and low SSC ("blast gate").
    • Mature Lymphocytes: Characterized by bright CD45 expression and low SSC.
    • Monocytes: Intermediate CD45 expression and moderate SSC.
    • Granulocytes: Intermediate CD45 expression and high SSC.

Lineage Assignment Panels

  • Precursor / Immaturity Antigens: $\text{CD34}$ (hematopoietic stem/progenitor cell antigen), $\text{TdT}$ (intranuclear polymerase), and $\text{CD10}$ (Common ALL Antigen [CALLA]).
  • Pan-B-Cell Antigens: $\text{CD19}$ (pan-B marker), $\text{CD20}$ (mature surface antigen; dim in CLL, bright in B-cell lymphomas), $\text{CD22}$, and cytoplasmic $\text{CD79a}$.
  • Pan-T-Cell Antigens: Cytoplasmic and surface $\text{CD3}$ (definitive lineage-specific marker for T-cells), $\text{CD2}$, $\text{CD5}$, and $\text{CD7}$.
  • NK-Cell Antigens: $\text{CD16}$ and $\text{CD56}$, in the strict absence of surface $\text{CD3}$.

B-Cell Clonality (Light Chain Restriction)

Normal polyclonal B-cells express either kappa ($\kappa$) or lambda ($\lambda$) immunoglobulin light chains on their cell membrane in a controlled balance, generating a normal surface ratio:

$$\text{Normal Surface }\kappa:\lambda\text{ Ratio} = 1.0\text{ -- }2.0:1 \quad (\approx 1.5:1)$$


Because neoplastic transformation represents the clonal outgrowth of a single transformed cell, all neoplastic daughter cells express the same light chain:

  • Kappa Light Chain Restriction: Gated B-cells demonstrate $> 90\text{%}$ kappa and $< 10\text{%}$ lambda expression, yielding a $\kappa:\lambda$ ratio $> 3:1$ (frequently $> 10:1$).
  • Lambda Light Chain Restriction: Gated B-cells demonstrate dominant lambda expression, driving the $\kappa:\lambda$ ratio $< 0.5:1$ (frequently $< 0.1:1$).

T-Cell Clonality (Aberrant Antigen Expression)

Because T-cells do not express surface light chains, flow cytometric identification of T-cell neoplasia relies on identifying an aberrant immunophenotype:

  1. Pan-T-cell Antigen Deletion: Loss of one or more pan-T-cell antigens (e.g., cells that express CD2, CD3, and CD5, but demonstrate complete downregulation or absence of CD7).
  2. Skewed CD4:CD8 Ratio: The normal helper-to-cytotoxic T-cell ratio ranges from $1.5\text{ -- }2.5:1$. Neoplastic expansions show extreme divergence ($> 10:1$ in Sézary syndrome or $< 0.1:1$ in cytotoxic T-LGL leukemia).

Pre-Analytical Requirements and Limitations

  • Anticoagulants and Storage: Peripheral blood or bone marrow aspirates must be collected in $\text{K}_2\text{EDTA}$ (lavender top) or Sodium Heparin (green top). Samples must be maintained at room temperature ($18\text{ -- }25^\circ\text{C}$, never frozen or refrigerated) and analyzed within $24\text{ -- }48\text{ hours}$. Cell viability must exceed $85\text{%}$ (assessed by 7-AAD or trypan blue exclusion) to prevent non-specific antibody adherence.
  • Inability to Assess Architecture: Flow cytometry operates exclusively on single-cell fluid suspensions and cannot evaluate bone marrow spatial infiltration patterns (e.g., paratrabecular vs. interstitial).
  • Classic Hodgkin Lymphoma Limitation: The diagnostic multinucleated Reed-Sternberg cells comprise $< 1\text{%}$ of the total cell mass in an involved node or marrow specimen and are mechanically fragile, rupturing during fluid suspension processing. Flow cytometry is not reliable for diagnosing Classic Hodgkin Lymphoma. Diagnosis requires fixed-tissue core biopsies and immunohistochemistry.

Diagnostic Methodologies

  • Conventional G-Band Karyotyping: Viable mononuclear cells are cultured and arrested in metaphase using mitotic spindle inhibitors (colchicine/colcemid). Following hypotonic treatment and methanol/acetic acid fixation, chromosomes are dropped onto slides, treated with trypsin, and stained with Giemsa. This permits global assessment of all 46 chromosomes, detecting numerical aneuploidies and large structural abnormalities. Actively dividing cells are required.
  • Fluorescence In Situ Hybridization (FISH): Employs fluorochrome-labeled DNA probes that hybridize to complementary genomic sequences. FISH is performed directly on non-dividing interphase nuclei, bypassing cell culture. Break-apart probes detect rearrangements involving a specific gene locus regardless of the fusion partner; dual-color dual-fusion probes confirm reciprocal translocations.
  • Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR / RT-qPCR): Amplifies targeted DNA or reverse-transcribed RNA (cDNA). Demonstrates clonality by analyzing the size distribution of immunoglobulin ($IGH$) or T-cell receptor ($TCR$) gene rearrangements: polyclonal populations produce a broad smear, whereas monoclonal expansions produce a sharp, discrete band. Reverse-transcription quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) tracks minimal residual disease (MRD) down to $1 \times 10^{-6}$.
  • Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS): High-throughput massively parallel sequencing that targets multi-gene panels to detect single nucleotide variants (SNVs) and small insertions/deletions across key oncogenes and tumor suppressor genes.

Most mature B-cell neoplasms harbor balanced reciprocal translocations involving the Immunoglobulin Heavy Chain locus ($IGH$) on chromosome 14q32. Juxtaposing strong immunoglobulin transcriptional enhancers next to oncogenes drives deregulation of cellular proliferation or survival.

  • Burkitt Lymphoma: $t(8;14)(q24;q32)$:
    • Translocates the $MYC$ proto-oncogene on 8q24 to the $IGH$ locus on 14q32.
    • Drives high-level constitutive expression of the MYC transcription factor, resulting in rapid proliferation with a Ki-67 proliferative index approaching $100\text{%}$.
  • Mantle Cell Lymphoma: $t(11;14)(q13;q32)$:
    • Translocates the Cyclin D1 gene (CCND1) on 11q13 to the $IGH$ locus on 14q32.
    • Produces nuclear accumulation of Cyclin D1, which overcomes the G1/S cell cycle checkpoint. Demonstrating $t(11;14)$ or nuclear Cyclin D1 overexpression via immunohistochemistry confirms the diagnosis.
  • Follicular Lymphoma: $t(14;18)(q32;q21)$:
    • Juxtaposes the anti-apoptotic gene $BCL2$ on 18q21 with the $IGH$ locus on 14q32.
    • BCL2 overexpression prevents physiological apoptosis in germinal center centrocytes and centroblasts, causing progressive accumulation of neoplastic cells.
  • Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia (B-ALL):
    • High-Risk: $t(9;22)(q34.1;q11.2)$ [BCR-ABL1 / Philadelphia Chromosome]: Creates a constitutively active tyrosine kinase fusion oncoprotein, conferring an aggressive baseline course that indicates treatment with targeted Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors (TKIs, e.g., imatinib, dasatinib).
    • High-Risk: $KMT2A$ ($MLL$) Gene Rearrangements at 11q23: Associated with therapy resistance, often seen in infant leukemias.
    • Favorable Risk: $t(12;21)(p13;q22)$ [ETV6-RUNX1]: A cryptic translocation invisible on standard G-band karyotypes that requires FISH or RT-PCR for detection. Associated with high cure rates in pediatric ALL.
    • Favorable Risk: Hyperdiploidy ($> 50$ chromosomes per metaphase): Responsive to standard antimetabolite therapy.

Specimen Management and Anticoagulant Rules

  • Living Mitotic Cultures (Karyotyping and Interphase FISH): Living cells are required. Bone marrow aspirates or peripheral blood must be collected in Sodium Heparin (green top). Samples must remain at ambient temperature ($18\text{ -- }25^\circ\text{C}$). Formalin, freezing, and EDTA exposure are strictly prohibited.
  • Molecular Diagnostic Assays (PCR, RT-qPCR, NGS): Mononuclear cells must be collected in $\text{K}_2\text{EDTA}$ (lavender top) or isolated frozen cell pellets. Heparin binds and inhibits DNA polymerases (Taq polymerase) used in PCR amplification and is unacceptable for molecular diagnostics.

Protein Electrophoresis and Immunofixation

  • Serum Protein Electrophoresis (SPEP): Serum proteins migrate through an agarose gel medium buffered at $\text{pH } 8.6$. Under these conditions, all proteins carry a net negative charge and migrate toward the anode, separating into five primary fractions: Albumin, $\alpha_1$-globulin, $\alpha_2$-globulin, $\beta$-globulin, and $\gamma$-globulin.
    • Monoclonal Spike (M-Spike): Appears as a tall, narrow, sharply defined, symmetrical peak within the $\gamma$ or $\beta$ migration zone.
    • Polyclonal Elevation: A broad, asymmetric, diffuse elevation across the gamma region characteristic of chronic inflammation, active hepatic disease, or autoimmune disorders.
  • Immunofixation Electrophoresis (IFE): Used to characterize the specific heavy- and light-chain components of the M-spike. Serum is electrophoresed across six parallel tracks, followed by incubation with monospecific antisera targeting IgG, IgA, IgM, $\kappa$, and $\lambda$. Monoclonal proteins precipitate as narrow, restricted bands where heavy- and light-chain lanes align (e.g., IgG kappa).

Quantitative Serum Free Light Chain (sFLC) Assays

  • Biochemical Principle: Nephelometric or turbidimetric immunoassays that use antibodies directed against epitopes exposed only when light chains are unbound to immunoglobulin heavy chains.
  • Diagnostic Sensitivity: Critical for identifying Light Chain Multiple Myeloma (representing $15\text{ -- }20\text{%}$ of myeloma cases), where malignant cells secrete unattached free light chains that are cleared rapidly by glomerular filtration and may be missed on standard SPEP.

  • Free Light Chain Ratio Calculation:

    $$\text{sFLC Ratio} = \frac{\text{Free }\kappa\text{ Light Chains (mg/L)}}{\text{Free }\lambda\text{ Light Chains (mg/L)}}$$

  • Reference Interval: $0.26\text{ -- }1.65$.

    • $\text{Ratio} > 1.65$: Indicates clonal kappa overproduction.
    • $\text{Ratio} < 0.26$: Indicates clonal lambda overproduction.
    • $\text{Ratio} \ge 100$ (with involved light chain $\ge 100\text{ mg/L}$): Serves as a validated myeloma-defining biomarker indicating immediate therapy.
  • Renal Function Adjustment: Because free light chains are cleared by glomerular filtration and metabolized in proximal renal tubules, renal impairment (elevated creatinine, reduced eGFR) prolongs their half-life, causing non-clonal increases in both light chains. In patients with renal failure, the diagnostic "renal reference interval" shifts to $0.37\text{ -- }3.1$. Technologists must correlate borderline elevations with serum creatinine values before reporting monoclonal restriction.

        • Acute leukemia (ALL): Precursor B-ALL vs Precursor T-ALL; blast threshold ($\ge 20\text{%}$), marrow failure (anemia, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia), mediastinal mass in T-ALL.
        • Chronic leukemia/lymphoma:
          • Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL): Monomorphic small mature lymphocytes, smudge cells (basket cells), absolute lymphocytosis ($\ge 5.0 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$), autoimmune hemolytic anemia (positive DAT).
          • Mantle Cell Lymphoma (MCL): Aggressive B-cell lymphoma, $t(11;14)$ CCND1::IGH Cyclin D1 overexpression.
          • Hairy Cell Leukemia (HCL): Indolent B-cell leukemia, pancytopenia, splenomegaly, dry tap (punctio sicca), hairy cells with circumferential cytoplasmic projections, BRAF V600E mutation.
        • Plasma cell dyscrasias:
          • Multiple Myeloma: Bone marrow clonal plasma cells ($\ge 10\text{%}$), CRAB criteria (Hypercalcemia, Renal failure, Anemia, Lytic bone lesions), monoclonal $M$-protein spike on SPEP, Bence Jones proteinuria, serum free light chains, prominent erythrocyte rouleaux on peripheral smear.
  • Hematology Laboratory Testing
    • Differentials and Morphology Evaluation (to include blood and body fluids): Differentiating lymphoblasts from myeloblasts; smudge cell recognition and preparation of 22% bovine serum albumin-treated smears; hairy cell identification; plasma cell morphology (eccentric nucleus, clock-face chromatin, basophilic cytoplasm, prominent perinuclear hof); peripheral blood rouleaux recognition.
    • Special Stains
      • Cytochemical stains: MPO/SBB negative in ALL; PAS block-like chunk positivity in B-ALL; Tartrate-Resistant Acid Phosphatase (TRAP) positive in Hairy Cell Leukemia.
    • Flow Cytometry Immunophenotyping
      • Leukemia & Lymphoma panels:
        • B-ALL: CD19+, CD10+ (CALLA), CD22+, CD79a+, CD34+, TdT+, CD45 dim.
        • T-ALL: Cytoplasmic CD3+ (lineage definitive), CD7+, CD2+, CD5+, CD1a+, CD4+, CD8+, TdT+.
        • CLL: CD19+, CD5+, CD23+, CD200+, dim surface immunoglobulin (sIg), CD20 dim, FMC-7 negative.
        • MCL: CD19+, CD5+, CD23 negative, bright surface immunoglobulin (sIg), FMC-7+.
        • HCL: CD19+, CD20 bright, CD11c+, CD25+, CD103+, CD123+, Annexin A1.
        • Multiple Myeloma: CD38 bright, CD138+, CD56 aberrant, CD19 negative, CD45 negative-to-dim, cytoplasmic light chain restriction ($\kappa : \lambda$).
    • Molecular and Cytogenetic Testing
      • Lymphoid genetics: B-ALL genetics ($t(12;21)$ ETV6::RUNX1, $t(9;22)$ BCR::ABL1, $t(4;11)$ KMT2A); MCL $t(11;14)$ CCND1::IGH; HCL BRAF V600E; CLL cytogenetics (17p deletion / TP53, 11q deletion, unmutated IGHV).

Laboratory Operations

  • Quality Assessment/Troubleshooting
    • Preanalytical, Analytical, Postanalytical: 22% albumin smear protocol to eliminate smudge cell artifacts; saline replacement technique to disperse true rouleaux vs cold agglutination; urgent critical reporting of acute blast leukemias.
